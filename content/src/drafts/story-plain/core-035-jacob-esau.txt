N: Jacob was on his way back to his homeland. Years earlier, he had crossed the Jordan River with nothing in his hand except a walking stick. Now he was returning with a large family, helpers, and herds of animals. He had sent messengers ahead to tell his brother Esau he was coming. Now those messengers returned with word.
S: GEN 32:6 @messengers after "saying,"
N: Four hundred men. That did not sound like a warm welcome. A heavy fear settled over Jacob. He did not know what his brother would do when they met. So Jacob split the people with him, along with his animals, into two separate camps. If Esau attacked one group, the other group might still have a chance to escape.
S: GEN 32:7
N: Making plans was not enough to calm his heart. Jacob turned to God in prayer. He remembered the God of his grandfather Abraham and his father Isaac. He remembered that God had told him to go back to his family and promised to treat him well. Jacob knew he did not deserve any of the kindness God had shown him.
S: GEN 32:9 @jacob after "And Jacob said,"
N: Jacob did not hide how scared he was. When people dread a hard meeting they cannot avoid, their minds often go straight to the worst thing that could happen. Jacob was honest with God about that dread. He prayed for safety for himself, for the mothers, and for the children. And he held onto God's promise that his family would grow beyond counting.
S: GEN 32:11 @jacob
N: Then the moment came. Jacob looked up across the open land and saw Esau coming toward them with his large company of men. Jacob placed his family in order. He put the two servant women and their children in front, Leah and her children behind them, and Rachel and Joseph at the very back.
S: GEN 33:1
N: Even after arranging everyone behind him, Jacob did not stay back and hide. He stepped out in front of them all and walked ahead to meet his brother. As he walked, he lowered himself all the way to the ground. He bowed seven times in all, showing deep respect until he stood near Esau.
S: GEN 33:3
N: Jacob had braced himself for anger. He had braced himself for a fight. Instead, Esau broke into a run.
S: GEN 33:4
N: The fear that had weighed on Jacob melted in the middle of the road. The two brothers held each other and cried. After a while, Esau looked up and saw the women and children waiting behind Jacob. When he asked who they were, Jacob told him they were the children God had kindly given him. One by one, the mothers and children came forward and bowed to Esau.
N: Esau also asked about the herds of animals he had met along the way. Jacob explained that they were meant as a gift, hoping his brother would look kindly on him. Esau did not want to take them.
S: GEN 33:9 @esau after "And Esau said,"
N: Still, Jacob wanted Esau to receive the gift. Seeing his brother welcome him with peace instead of anger felt like seeing a glimpse of God's own grace.
S: GEN 33:10 @jacob after "And Jacob said,"
N: Jacob had more than enough, and he knew it was because God had been good to him.
S: GEN 33:11 @jacob until "because I have enough."
N: Jacob kept asking until Esau finally took the gift. That was how their meeting ended. There was no attack from all those men. There were no mothers and children running for their lives. Instead, two brothers stood in peace on the open road. Sometimes the very meeting we fear the most becomes the place where grace meets us first.
