N: Jesus crossed to the far side of the Sea of Galilee, a wide lake also called Tiberias. He was not alone for long. People had heard about the sick being healed, and a huge crowd followed him around the water.
S: JOH 6:2
N: It was almost time for the Passover, a great Jewish feast. Jesus walked up onto a mountain with his disciples and sat down to rest with them there.
S: JOH 6:3
N: From that high place, they could see across the slopes. Jesus looked up and watched a large crowd walking toward them. He knew they were far from home and would need something to eat. So he turned to Philip and asked where they could buy bread for all these people.
S: JOH 6:5
N: Jesus asked this to test Philip's faith. Jesus already knew what he was going to do. Philip looked out over the crowd and counted the cost in his head. Even a large sum of money would not buy enough for everyone to have a single bite.
S: JOH 6:7 @philip after "Philip answered him,"
N: It is easy to understand why Philip felt that way. Sometimes we look at what we have in our hands. It feels far too small for the need in front of us.
N: Then Andrew, Simon Peter's brother, spoke up. He had spotted a young boy in the crowd who happened to have some food with him.
S: JOH 6:9 @andrew
N: Five loaves of barley bread and two little fish. Barley was simple, everyday bread. One boy's meal was barely enough for a single person, let alone a mountainside full of hungry people. Andrew offered what he had found, even though he could not see how it would help.
N: Jesus did not send anyone away. He told his disciples to have the people sit down. Thick green grass covered the ground there, making a soft place for weary travelers to rest. About five thousand men sat down across the grass.
S: JOH 6:10
N: Everything grew quiet on the hillside. Jesus took the loaves in his hands and thanked God for them. Then he began to share the bread with his disciples, and they walked out among the people to pass it around. He did the same with the fish. Everyone ate as much as they wanted.
S: JOH 6:11
N: Nobody had to hold back or worry that the food would run out. Every person ate until they were full. When the meal was over, Jesus told his disciples not to let any of the extra food go to waste.
S: JOH 6:12
N: So the disciples walked back across the grass, gathering up the leftover pieces of barley bread. When they were done, they had filled twelve baskets with what remained. There was far more left over than what they had started with.
S: JOH 6:13
N: The men looked at those twelve full baskets. They remembered how hungry they had been, and how little there had seemed to be. They began to talk to one another about the one who had fed them.
S: JOH 6:14 @men after "said,"
N: The boy only had five small loaves and two fish. Philip only saw an impossible bill to pay. Yet when that small meal was placed in Jesus' hands, he thanked God and made it more than enough. That is where the story leaves us, with a quiet hillside and twelve full baskets. Nothing is too small when it is placed in his hands.
