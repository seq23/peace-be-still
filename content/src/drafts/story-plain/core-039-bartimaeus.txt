N: Jesus was on the road again, moving through Jericho with his disciples and a large crowd. Everywhere he went, people followed him. The noise of that crowd would have carried far down the road. Dust rose from all those feet on the dry earth.
S: MAR 10:46
N: Bartimaeus could not see the crowd, only hear it. He sat in his usual place beside the road, begging for what little people might spare. Most days, people walked right past him without stopping.
N: Then he heard who was passing by. Word was moving through the travelers that Jesus of Nazareth was on the road. Bartimaeus had never seen his face, but he knew enough to hope. He did not wait for someone to notice him. He lifted his voice above the noise.
S: MAR 10:47 @bartimaeus after "and say,"
N: The people walking near him did not want the shouting. Perhaps they thought a beggar had no business calling out to a teacher. Perhaps they just wanted to keep moving. They told him to be quiet. Bartimaeus did not listen. He knew this moment would pass in seconds, and he might never get another one. So he cried out even louder.
S: MAR 10:48 @bartimaeus after "great deal,"
N: A crowd can be loud enough to drown out one voice. Yet Jesus heard him. In the middle of all those footsteps and talking people, Jesus stopped walking.
S: MAR 10:49 until "called."
N: Everything changed in a moment. The same people who had just told Bartimaeus to hush were now turning toward him. They told him to take heart and get to his feet, because Jesus was asking for him.
S: MAR 10:49 @crowd after "saying unto him,"
N: Bartimaeus did not hesitate. He had an outer cloak around his shoulders. He threw it off so nothing would trip him or slow him down. In the dark, guided by the sound of voices, he stood up and made his way to Jesus.
S: MAR 10:50
N: Now Bartimaeus was standing right in front of him. Everyone there could probably guess what a blind beggar wanted. Even so, Jesus did not assume. He asked him directly.
S: MAR 10:51 until "unto thee?"
N: It was a simple, open question. Jesus gave him room to speak for himself, with no one shushing him now. Bartimaeus did not ask for silver, or for an easier place to sit. He asked for the one thing he could not give himself.
S: MAR 10:51 @bartimaeus after "The blind man said unto him,"
N: Jesus did not make him wait. He answered him right there on the road.
S: MAR 10:52
N: At once, the dark lifted, and Bartimaeus could see. Notice what he did next. Jesus told him he was free to go his own way. Instead, Bartimaeus chose to walk down the road behind Jesus. The man who had sat blind in the dust beside the road was now walking on it, seeing clearly, following the one who had stopped for him. That is the quiet truth the story leaves with us. No crowd is too loud, and no person at the edge of the road is too small, for Jesus to stop and listen.
