# Plain telling, core stories 21-30.

=== core-021-adam-eve | Adam and Eve in the Garden

shelf: adult
violence: none
sexual: none
cast: adam|Adam|adult|m; eve|Eve|adult|f; serpent|serpent|adult|m
N: God shaped the first man out of the earth and gave him breath, and the man became a living soul. Then God planted a garden in Eden, toward the east, and put the man there to work it and keep it. Every tree that was good to look at and good for food grew there.
N: God gave the man one boundary. He could eat freely from every tree in the garden except one: the tree that gave the knowledge of good and evil. If he ate from that tree, he would die.
S: GEN 2:18
N: God brought the animals to the man, and the man gave each one its name. But among them all, there was no partner suited to him. So God caused a deep sleep to fall on him, took one of his ribs, and made a woman, and brought her to the man.

N: They were together in the garden, and they had nothing to hide from each other or from God.
S: GEN 3:1 @serpent
N: Notice how the question twists what God said. God had given them every tree but one. The serpent made it sound as if God had held everything back.
N: The woman answered that they could eat from the trees, but not from the tree in the middle of the garden, or they would die.
S: GEN 3:4 @serpent
S: GEN 3:5 @serpent
S: GEN 3:6
N: In a single moment, everything changed. Their eyes were opened, and they knew they were naked. They sewed fig leaves together to cover themselves. Then, in the cool of the day, they heard God walking in the garden, and they hid from him among the trees.
S: GEN 3:9
N: God knew where Adam was. The question was an invitation to come out.
S: GEN 3:10 @adam
N: God asked whether he had eaten from the tree he had been told not to eat from.
S: GEN 3:12 @adam
S: GEN 3:13 @eve after "And the woman said, "
N: The man blamed the woman, and even seemed to blame God for giving her to him. The woman blamed the serpent. People have been passing blame ever since.
N: There were consequences. Life outside the garden would be hard. Work would be sweat and struggle, and in the end they would return to the dust they came from. Adam named his wife Eve, the mother of everyone who would ever live.
S: GEN 3:21
N: Before they left the garden, God made them clothes of skins and dressed them himself. Their choices were real, and so was their loss. But even as they went out of Eden, God was still caring for them.

N: Every year the family went up to Shiloh to worship the LORD and to offer sacrifices. Elkanah gave portions to Peninnah and all her sons and daughters. N: Elkanah loved her, and he meant well. But his love could not reach the ache she carried. Some sorrows only God can hold.
N: One year, after they had eaten in Shiloh, Hannah got up and went to the house of the LORD. Eli the priest was sitting near the doorpost of the temple.
S: 1SA 1:10
S: 1SA 1:11 @hannah
N: Hannah prayed silently. Her lips moved, but no sound came out. Eli was watching her mouth, and he thought she was drunk. He told her to put away her wine.
S: 1SA 1:15 @hannah
N: Hannah did not hide her heart. She called herself a woman of a sorrowful spirit. She told the priest she had poured out her soul before the LORD. She told him she had been speaking out of the depth of her grief.
S: 1SA 1:17 @eli
S: 1SA 1:18 @hannah until "So the woman"
N: Nothing had changed yet. There was no child, and no sign. But Hannah went her way, ate, and her face was no longer sad. She had laid her burden down before God, and she walked away lighter.
N: Early the next morning, the family worshipped before the LORD and went home to Ramah.
S: 1SA 1:20

N: Hannah's story does not promise that every prayer for a child will be answered with a child. Many faithful people have prayed that prayer and waited a long time, or are waiting still. What the story shows is a woman who brought her whole grief to God, honestly, and found peace before anything changed.

=== core-024-through-the-roof | The Friends Who Opened the Roof
passage: MAR 2:1-12
shelf: adult
violence: none
sexual: none
cast: scribes|the scribes|adult|group; crowd|they all|adult|group
N: Jesus came back to Capernaum, and word spread that he was in a house there. So many people gathered that there was no room left, not even around the door. Jesus spoke the word to them.
S: MAR 2:3
N: Four friends carried a man who could not walk. They carried him on a mat, hoping to get him to Jesus. But when they arrived, they could not get through the crowd.
S: MAR 2:4
N: Most people would have turned back. These friends climbed up onto the roof and opened it right above where Jesus was. Imagine the dust and pieces falling, everyone looking up, and then a man on a mat being lowered down on ropes, right into the middle of the room.
S: MAR 2:5
N: Notice whose faith Jesus saw. Their faith. The man on the mat could not get himself there. His friends carried him, refused to give up, and found another way in. Sometimes faith looks like friends who will carry you when you cannot walk.
N: But Jesus said something no one expected. Before he said anything about the man's legs, he said his sins were forgiven.
S: MAR 2:6
S: MAR 2:7 @scribes whole
N: Jesus knew what they were thinking. He asked them which was easier, to tell this man his sins were forgiven, or to tell him to get up and walk. Then he said he would show them that the Son of man had power on earth to forgive sins.
S: MAR 2:11
S: MAR 2:12 @crowd
N: The man who was carried in walked out, carrying his own mat. He went out in front of all of them. The crowd that had blocked the door now stepped aside to let him through.
N: This story gives us two pictures. One is of Jesus, who sees the whole person and speaks to the deepest need first. The other is of four friends whose names we never learn. They did not preach or argue. They carried, they climbed, they made a hole in a roof. And Jesus saw their faith.

N: Her husband had been one of the prophets, a man who feared the LORD. Now he was gone, and he had left a debt she could not pay. The creditor was coming to take her two sons as bondmen to work off what was owed.
N: She did not sit quietly with her trouble. She cried out to the prophet Elisha.
S: 2KI 4:2 @elisha until "And she said"

S: 2KI 4:3 @elisha

N: It was a strange instruction. Borrow empty jars from all the neighbors. Not a few. Then go inside, close the door, and start pouring.
N: Think of what that meant. She had to go door to door and ask. Her neighbors would see her need. Then she had to trust God behind a closed door, with only her sons as witnesses.
S: 2KI 4:5
S: 2KI 4:6

S: 2KI 4:7 @elisha
