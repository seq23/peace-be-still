# Plain telling, core stories 11-20.

=== core-011-noah | Noah and the Rainbow
passage: GEN 6:5-22; GEN 7:1-24; GEN 8:1-22; GEN 9:8-17
shelf: adult
violence: mild
sexual: none
cast:
N: In the days of Noah, the earth had grown violent and corrupt. The Bible says it grieved the LORD in his heart. But one man stood apart. The LORD looked on Noah with favor. Noah walked with God.
N: God told Noah what was coming. A flood would cover the earth. Noah was to build an ark of gopher wood, a great boat with rooms inside, sealed with pitch inside and out. He was to bring his wife, his three sons and their wives, and two of every kind of living creature, male and female, with food for them all.
S: GEN 6:22
N: Picture the work of it. Cutting and fitting and sealing, day after day. Noah did not have a forecast. He had a word from God, and he built on it.
N: When the ark was ready, the animals came, two by two. Noah's family went inside. Then something tender happens in the story.
S: GEN 7:16
N: The LORD shut him in. Noah did not have to close that great door himself. The rain fell for forty days and forty nights. The waters rose until even the mountains were covered. Everything outside the ark was lost. Inside, a family and a boat full of animals rode on the water, waiting.
S: GEN 8:1
N: God remembered Noah. The wind passed over the earth, and slowly the waters went down. The ark came to rest on the mountains of Ararat. Still Noah waited. After forty more days he opened the window and sent out a raven, and then a dove. The dove found no place to rest, so it came back to his hand. Seven days later, he sent her out again.
S: GEN 8:11
N: A single fresh leaf. That was how Noah knew the water was going down. Sometimes the first sign of a new season is very small.
N: When the earth was dry, God told Noah to bring everyone out. Noah built an altar and worshipped. Then God made a promise, not only to Noah, but to every living creature.
S: GEN 9:13
S: GEN 9:15
N: The rainbow is a sign of that promise. God set it in the clouds so that whenever it appears, he will remember his covenant. The next time you see one after a storm, you can remember this story: a family kept safe through the flood, a dove with a leaf, and a God who remembers.

=== core-012-esther | Esther, for Such a Time as This
passage: EST 2:5-17; EST 3:8-13; EST 4:1-17; EST 5:1-3
shelf: adult
violence: mild
sexual: none
cast: esther|Esther|adult|f; mordecai|Mordecai|elder|m; king|the king|adult|m
N: Esther was a young Jewish woman living in the Persian capital of Shushan. Her parents had died, and her cousin Mordecai had raised her as his own daughter. In time, she was chosen as queen by King Ahasuerus. But on Mordecai's advice, she had never told anyone at the palace who her people were.
N: Then a powerful official named Haman persuaded the king to sign a terrible order. On a single day, the Jews in every province of the kingdom were to be destroyed. Letters went out with the king's seal.
N: When Mordecai heard, he tore his clothes and put on sackcloth and ashes. He cried with a loud and bitter cry in the middle of the city. Across the provinces, Jewish families fasted and wept.
N: Esther sent a servant to find out what was wrong. Mordecai sent back a copy of the order and asked her to go to the king and plead for her people.
N: But there was a problem. Anyone who went in to the king without being called could be put to death, unless the king held out his golden sceptre. And Esther had not been called in thirty days.
S: EST 4:14 @mordecai
N: For such a time as this. Mordecai did not promise Esther it would be easy or safe. He asked her to consider that her place in the palace might be part of something bigger than her own comfort.
S: EST 4:16 @esther
N: If I perish, I perish. Esther did not walk in alone. She asked her people to fast with her for three days, and she and her maidens fasted too. Then she chose to go.
N: On the third day, Esther put on her royal robes and went to stand in the king's inner court. Imagine the silence. Every step could have been her last.
S: EST 5:2
S: EST 5:3 @king
N: The king held out the sceptre. Esther came near and touched the top of it. The door that could have closed on her opened instead. Her courage began a turning that would save her people.
N: Esther's story is not about being fearless. It is about being afraid and going anyway, after prayer and fasting, with others standing with her. She used the place she had been given for the good of her people.

=== core-013-job | Job, Who Would Not Let Go
passage: JOB 1:1-22; JOB 2:7-13; JOB 42:10-17
shelf: adult
violence: mild
sexual: none
cast: job|Job|adult|m
S: JOB 1:1
N: Job lived in the land of Uz. He had seven sons and three daughters, great flocks and herds, and many servants. He was known as the greatest man in all the east. He loved his children and prayed for them.
N: Then, in a single day, it all fell apart. One messenger after another came running. Raiders had taken the oxen and the donkeys. Fire had fallen and burned up the sheep. The camels were gone. And while one messenger was still speaking, another came with the worst news of all. A great wind had struck the house where Job's children were gathered, and they had died.
N: Job stood up and tore his robe. He shaved his head. He fell down on the ground and worshipped.
S: JOB 1:21 @job
S: JOB 1:22
N: Job was not pretending he felt no pain. He tore his robe. He sat on the ground. But in the middle of the worst day of his life, he still turned toward God.
N: Then Job's own body was struck with painful sores from the soles of his feet to the top of his head. He sat down among the ashes. Three friends heard what had happened and came to comfort him. When they saw him, they barely knew him. They wept aloud.
S: JOB 2:13
N: Seven days of silence. Before Job's friends said anything that went wrong, they did something right. They sat with him and did not try to explain his grief.
N: The book of Job is long. Job asks God hard questions, and his friends give him hard answers. In the end, God speaks, and Job is humbled before him. The book never gives a simple reason for Job's suffering. What it shows is a man who would not let go of God, even when he did not understand.
S: JOB 42:10
N: Job prayed for the very friends who had hurt him with their words. Then the LORD turned his captivity. His family came to comfort him. In time he had seven sons and three daughters again, and he lived to see his grandchildren and great-grandchildren.
N: Job's restored life did not erase his grief. Nothing in the story says it did. But it tells us that the God Job held on to through the dark had not let go of him either.

=== core-014-creation | In the Beginning
passage: GEN 1:1-31; GEN 2:1-3
shelf: adult
violence: none
sexual: none
cast:
S: GEN 1:1
S: GEN 1:2
N: Before anything else, there was God. The earth was without shape and empty, and darkness covered the deep waters. But even there, in the dark, the Spirit of God was moving.
S: GEN 1:3
N: The first thing God made was light. He saw that it was good, and he divided the light from the darkness. He named the light Day and named the darkness Night. Evening came, then morning, and that was the first day.
N: On the second day, God made the sky, the wide firmament, dividing the waters above from the waters below. On the third day, he gathered the seas into one place, and dry land appeared. Then the land grew green. Grass came up, and plants with seed, and trees with fruit, each after its own kind. God looked, and it was good.
N: On the fourth day, God set lights in the sky: the sun to rule the day, the moon to rule the night, and the stars as well. On the fifth day, he filled the waters with living creatures, and the air with birds of every kind. He blessed them and told them to be fruitful and multiply.
N: On the sixth day, God made the animals of the land: cattle, creeping things, and wild beasts, each after its own kind. And then he did something new.
S: GEN 1:27
N: People were made in the image of God, male and female. Every person you will ever meet carries that image. God blessed them and gave them the care of the earth, its fish and birds and living things, and gave them plants and fruit for food.
S: GEN 1:31
N: Notice the difference. After each day, God saw that it was good. When he looked at everything together, with people in it, he saw that it was very good.
S: GEN 2:2
N: On the seventh day, God rested. He blessed that day and set it apart. The story of creation ends not with more work but with rest.
N: The world began with light coming into darkness, with order coming to what was empty, and with a God who called it good. At the end of a long day, it is a good story to remember. Light first. Then everything else. Then rest.

N: Moses was a long way from the palace where he had grown up. He had fled Egypt years before. Now he was a shepherd in the land of Midian, keeping the flock of his father-in-law, Jethro. One day he led the sheep to the far side of the wilderness and came to Horeb, the mountain of God.
S: EXO 3:2
N: A bush on fire in the desert was not unusual. But this one kept burning, and it was not burned up.
S: EXO 3:3 @moses
N: Moses had to stop and turn aside to look. Sometimes the holy moments in our lives begin when we notice something and stop to look.
S: EXO 3:4
S: EXO 3:5
N: Moses took off his sandals. Ordinary desert dirt had become holy ground, because God was there. Then God told Moses who was speaking. He was the God of Moses's father, and of Abraham, Isaac and Jacob before him. Moses covered his face. He was afraid to look upon God.
S: EXO 3:7
N: I have seen. I have heard. I know. God had not forgotten his people in Egypt. Their cries had reached him. He told Moses he had come down to bring them out of Egypt and into a good land. Then he told Moses that he was sending him to Pharaoh.
S: EXO 3:11 @moses
N: Who am I? It is the question many of us ask when we are called to something bigger than ourselves. Moses had been away for years. He was a shepherd now. He did not feel like a leader.
N: God did not answer by telling Moses how capable he was. He answered by telling Moses that he would be with him. Then Moses asked another question. If the people asked him the name of the God who sent him, what should he say?
S: EXO 3:14

N: Moses still had more questions, and more doubts, in the chapters ahead. But this was where it began: a shepherd, a bush that would not burn up, and a God who had seen the suffering of his people and was coming to help them.

=== core-019-mary-martha | Mary and Martha
passage: LUK 10:38-42
shelf: adult
violence: none
sexual: none
cast: martha|Martha|adult|f
S: LUK 10:38
N: Jesus was traveling with his disciples, and they came to a village. A woman named Martha opened her home to him. Hospitality mattered deeply. When a guest came, especially an honored teacher with his followers, there was food to prepare, water to draw and a home to make ready.
S: LUK 10:39
N: Martha had a sister named Mary. While Martha worked, Mary sat at the feet of Jesus and listened to him teach. She stayed close and took in every word.
N: Imagine Martha's afternoon. Pots on the fire. Bread to bake. Guests to feed. She moved from task to task, and every time she passed the doorway, there was her sister, sitting and listening.
S: LUK 10:40 @martha
N: Martha did what many of us would do. She brought her frustration straight to Jesus. Lord, do you not care? It is the same question the disciples asked in the storm. It comes from a tired heart that feels alone with too much to do.
N: Notice that Jesus did not scold her for serving. Serving is good. Feeding people is good. Opening your home is good. The trouble was not Martha's work. It was the worry and the weight she was carrying while she did it.
S: LUK 10:41
S: LUK 10:42
N: Martha, Martha. He said her name twice, gently, the way you speak to someone you love. He saw how careful and troubled she was about many things. And he told her only one thing was needed.
N: Mary had chosen to sit with him and listen. Jesus would not take that away from her. He was not asking Martha to stop caring for people. He was inviting her to come and sit down too.
N: Most of us know Martha's day. The list that never ends, the feeling that everyone else is resting while we work. This story does not make Martha the villain. It shows Jesus seeing a busy, worried woman and inviting her to rest at his feet.
N: The story ends there, with the invitation still open. We do not know whether Martha set down her work that day. But we know what Jesus offered her. He offered the good part, the one thing that is needed.

S: 1KI 17:12 @widow

S: 1KI 17:13 @elijah

S: 1KI 17:15
S: 1KI 17:16

N: This story is about God's care for a poor widow and her child in a foreign land, in the middle of a drought. She was not an Israelite. She was not wealthy or powerful. But God sent his prophet to her house and kept her family through the hard season.
