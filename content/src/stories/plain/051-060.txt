=== core-053-lost-sheep | The Lost Sheep
passage: LUK 15:1-7
shelf: adult
violence: none
sexual: none
cast: pharisees|Pharisees|adult|group
N: Wherever Jesus went, all kinds of people came close to listen. Some of them were the people everyone else stayed away from.
S: LUK 15:1
N: Publicans were tax collectors who worked for Rome. Most people saw them as traitors and cheats. Sinners meant anyone known for living wrong. And Jesus did not keep them at a distance. He sat down and ate with them.
S: LUK 15:2 @pharisees after "murmured, saying, "
N: The religious leaders meant it as a complaint. They could not understand why a teacher of God would sit at that table. So Jesus told them a story.
S: LUK 15:3
S: LUK 15:4
N: A hundred sheep is a lot of sheep to look after. Losing one might not seem like much. A careful businessman might say, ninety-nine is good enough. But a shepherd does not think that way. He knows each one.
N: So he goes out. Over the rocks, through the brush, calling into the dark, until he hears that one small cry. It may take all night. The ninety-nine are safe together. The one alone is the one in danger, and the shepherd will not rest until it is found.
S: LUK 15:5
N: He does not scold the sheep. He does not make it walk home on its own. He picks it up and carries it across his shoulders, and he is glad the whole way back.
S: LUK 15:6
N: He cannot keep the joy to himself. He calls his friends and neighbors and throws a celebration for one lost sheep.
S: LUK 15:7
N: The people who were grumbling thought the ones at the table were too far gone. Jesus told them heaven sees it the other way. When one person who wandered off comes home, heaven does not shrug. Heaven rejoices.
N: This is one of three stories Jesus told that day. A lost sheep, a lost coin, and a lost son. Each one ends the same way, with something found and a party thrown.
N: Some people hear this story and think of someone they love who has wandered off. Some people hear it and know they are the one that is lost. Either way, the story is clear about the shepherd. He does not wait for the sheep to find its own way back. He goes looking, and when he finds it, he carries it home with joy.
