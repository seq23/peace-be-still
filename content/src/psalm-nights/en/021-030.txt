=== pn-21-psalm-127 | Psalm 127: He Giveth His Beloved Sleep
main: PSA 127:1-2
companions: PSA 4:8; MAT 6:25-27; MAT 6:34; 1PE 5:7
welcome: Hello, and welcome to Psalm Nights. [pause 22]
welcome: Put down whatever you were holding. Let your arms rest beside you. [pause 25]
welcome: Tonight's psalm is a short one, Psalm 127. Only two verses tonight. But they are two verses for anyone who works too hard and worries too late. [pause 24]
welcome: We will read them slowly, and then again. [pause 25]
reflect1: Except the Lord build the house. We can work and plan and stay up late, and still the real building belongs to God. [pause 34]
reflect1: Then the line about bread. Eating the bread of sorrows. That is worry, chewed over and over, late into the night. [pause 34]
reflect1: And then, so gently. He giveth his beloved sleep. Beloved. That is what he calls you. [pause 34]
reflect1: Some companion verses now, from the words of Jesus and from Peter. [pause 32]
reflect2: Jesus pointed to the birds of the air. They do not plant or store up food, and still they are fed. Which of you, he asked, can add one hour to your life by worrying? [pause 34]
reflect2: Tomorrow can take care of itself. Today's trouble is enough for today. [pause 34]
reflect2: And Peter says, cast all your care on him, because he cares for you. All of it. Even the small worries. Even the ones you are embarrassed by. [pause 34]
reflect2: Here is Psalm 127 again. Two verses. Let the word beloved be the last one you hear. [pause 34]
rest: rest6

=== pn-22-psalm-130 | Psalm 130: Waiting for the Morning
main: PSA 130:1-8
companions: LAM 3:25-26
welcome: Welcome to Psalm Nights. [pause 22]
welcome: Let the dark be what it is. You do not have to fill it. [pause 25]
welcome: Tonight's psalm is Psalm 130. It begins out of the depths. It is a psalm for the low places, and for long nights of waiting for morning. [pause 24]
welcome: We will read it twice. [pause 25]
reflect1: Out of the depths. The psalm does not wait until things are better to pray. It prays from the bottom. [pause 36]
reflect1: If thou shouldest mark iniquities, who shall stand? If God kept a record of every wrong, no one could stand. But there is forgiveness with him. [pause 36]
reflect1: Then the most beautiful line. Waiting for the Lord, more than the watchmen wait for morning. The night watchman on the city wall, tired, cold, looking east for the first gray light. [pause 36]
reflect1: The psalm says it twice. More than the watchmen. Like someone who knows morning is coming, and is waiting for it. [pause 36]
reflect1: A companion passage from Lamentations. [pause 34]
reflect2: The Lord is good to them that wait for him. It is good to hope, and to wait quietly. Quietly. You can wait quietly tonight. [pause 36]
reflect2: Here is Psalm 130 again. Be the watchman, waiting for the light. [pause 36]
rest: rest4

=== pn-23-psalm-32 | Psalm 32: Thou Art My Hiding Place
main: PSA 32:1-11
companions: 1JO 1:9
welcome: Hello. This is Psalm Nights. [pause 15]
welcome: Let your body settle. Let your breathing find its pace. [pause 15]
welcome: Tonight we read Psalm 32. It is a psalm about the weight of keeping something inside, and the relief of letting it out before God. [pause 15]
welcome: We will read it twice. [pause 15]
reflect1: Blessed is he whose transgression is forgiven. The psalm begins with relief. Happy is the one whose sin is covered. [pause 25]
reflect1: Then David remembers what silence cost him. While he kept silent, his bones grew old. Day and night, the weight was heavy on him. Some things we carry make us tired in the body too. [pause 27]
reflect1: And then he said it. I will confess. And the weight was lifted. [pause 25]
reflect1: Thou art my hiding place. Not hiding from God. Hiding in God. [pause 29]
reflect1: One companion verse. [pause 23]
reflect2: If we confess, he is faithful and just to forgive. If there is something on your heart tonight, you can tell him. He already knows, and he is not leaving. [pause 27]
reflect2: Here is Psalm 32 again. Listen for the hiding place. [pause 29]
rest: rest5

=== pn-25-psalm-37 | Psalm 37: Rest in the Lord
main: PSA 37:1-11
companions: PHI 4:6-7
welcome: Hello, and welcome to Psalm Nights. [pause 12]
welcome: Let your hands open. Let your forehead smooth out. [pause 15]
welcome: Tonight we read the first part of Psalm 37. It begins with a word we need some nights. Fret not. Do not get worked up. [pause 14]
welcome: We will read it twice. [pause 15]
reflect1: Fret not thyself. Some nights we lie awake upset about what others have done. People who did wrong and seem to get away with it. The psalm says, do not let it eat at you. [pause 23]
reflect1: Then four small instructions. Trust in the Lord. Delight thyself in him. Commit thy way. Rest in the Lord. [pause 21]
reflect1: Rest in the Lord, and wait for him. That is the whole night's work. Rest, and wait. [pause 21]
reflect1: And the meek shall inherit the earth. Jesus said it again, years later, on a mountain. [pause 25]
reflect1: A companion passage from Paul. [pause 19]
reflect2: Be careful for nothing. In the old English, careful means full of care. Full of worry. Instead, prayer, with thanks. And a peace that keeps the heart. [pause 23]
reflect2: Here is Psalm 37 again. When you hear rest in the Lord, let your body rest too. [pause 25]
rest: rest1

=== pn-28-psalm-57 | Psalm 57: In the Shadow of Thy Wings
main: PSA 57:1-11
companions: RUT 2:12
welcome: Welcome to Psalm Nights. [pause 14]
welcome: Let the covers be warm around you. [pause 15]
welcome: Tonight's psalm is Psalm 57. The heading says David wrote it when he hid from Saul in a cave. A dark cave, an enemy outside. And this is the song he sang there. [pause 15]
welcome: We will read it twice. [pause 15]
reflect1: Be merciful unto me, O God. Twice, at the start. Then, a refuge in the shadow of his wings, until the trouble has passed over. Until they pass. They will pass. [pause 27]
reflect1: In the middle, David describes lions. Danger all around. And then he lifts his eyes. God, be lifted high above the heavens. [pause 25]
reflect1: Then a line about the dawn. I myself will awake early. David in a cave, deciding to wake the morning with a song. [pause 29]
reflect1: A companion verse, from the story of Ruth. [pause 23]
reflect2: Boaz said it to Ruth, a foreign widow who had come with nothing. You have come to trust under his wings. The same wings David sang about. [pause 27]
reflect2: Here is Psalm 57 again. Picture yourself under those wings, until the night is overpast. [pause 29]

=== pn-30-psalm-31 | Psalm 31: Into Thine Hand
main: PSA 31:1-5
companions: PSA 31:14-15; LUK 23:46; PSA 4:8
welcome: Welcome to Psalm Nights. [pause 21]
welcome: Let this be the last thing you do today. Lie down. Listen. [pause 24]
welcome: Tonight we read the beginning of Psalm 31. It holds a line Jesus prayed on the cross, and a line many Christians have prayed at bedtime for centuries. Into thine hand I commit my spirit. [pause 23]
welcome: We will read it twice. [pause 25]
reflect1: In you, Lord, I trust. The psalm begins where every night can begin. Trust. [pause 33]
reflect1: A strong rock, a house of defence. A house. Somewhere to go in, and close the door. [pause 33]
reflect1: Then verse five. Into thine hand I commit my spirit. To commit is to hand something over and leave it there. Like a child handing a tired toy to her mother at the end of the day. [pause 34]
reflect1: Some companion verses now. [pause 31]
reflect2: My times are in thy hand. All of them. The past, this night, tomorrow. [pause 33]
reflect2: And Jesus, at the end of his suffering, prayed this same psalm. Father, into thy hands. If he could pray it then, you can pray it tonight. [pause 34]
reflect2: And our evening verse, one last time. Peace. Lying down. Sleep. [pause 33]
reflect2: Here is Psalm 31 again. When you hear verse five, hand him your spirit for the night. [pause 34]
rest: rest6
