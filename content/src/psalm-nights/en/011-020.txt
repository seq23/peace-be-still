# Psalm Nights 11-20 (English, KJV). Written by Claude (no free-model draft), with Claude's pastor and cultural read. New shared rests: _rest4, _rest5.

=== _rest4

text: Turn a little, if you want to, until your body finds the place it likes. [pause 10]
text: There is nothing you need to remember. Nothing you need to say back. [pause 12]
text: If someone you love is far away tonight, you can leave them in God's hands too. [pause 15]
text: Breathe out slowly. Then again. [pause 20]
text: The night is held. You are held. [pause 20]
text: Sleep well. [pause 45]

=== pn-11-psalm-16 | Psalm 16: The Path of Life
main: PSA 16:1-11
companions: PSA 4:8; PSA 73:25-26
welcome: Hello, and welcome to Psalm Nights. [pause 12]
welcome: Let your body sink into the bed. Let your feet be warm. [pause 15]
welcome: Tonight's psalm is Psalm 16. It is a psalm of contentment. Of someone who looks at their life and says, the lines have fallen for me in pleasant places. [pause 14]
welcome: We will read it twice, slowly. [pause 15]
reflect1: Preserve me, O God. The psalm begins with a short prayer. Keep me. [pause 18]
reflect1: In the middle there is a line about the night. My reins instruct me, in the night seasons. The old word reins means the deep places inside. Even at night, God is teaching the heart. [pause 18]
reflect1: Pleasant places, the psalm says. In those days the land was measured out with lines. The psalmist looks at his portion and is glad. Not because it is the biggest. Because the Lord is in it. [pause 20]
reflect1: And at the end, fulness of joy, in his presence. [pause 22]
reflect1: Two companion passages. [pause 16]
reflect2: The first is the evening verse we often return to. Peace, lying down, sleep. [pause 18]
reflect2: The second is from Psalm 73. My flesh and my heart may fail. God is the strength of my heart. When your body is tired and your heart is worn, he is still your portion. [pause 20]
reflect2: Here is Psalm 16 again. Listen for the pleasant places. [pause 22]
rest: rest4

=== pn-12-psalm-34 | Psalm 34: Taste and See
main: PSA 34:1-10
companions: PSA 34:17-18
welcome: Welcome to Psalm Nights. [pause 16]
welcome: Let the lights go down. Let your shoulders go down with them. [pause 19]
welcome: Tonight we read the first part of Psalm 34. David wrote it after a frightening escape, when he had to pretend to be out of his mind to get away. And afterward, he sang. [pause 18]
welcome: We will read it twice. [pause 21]
reflect1: Bless the Lord, at all times. At all times. Not only when the day went well. [pause 31]
reflect1: He sought the Lord, and was heard. David is not giving a lecture here. He is telling what happened to him. I looked for God, and God answered. [pause 31]
reflect1: The angel of the Lord encamps around those who fear him. Encamps. Like a tent pitched around you for the night. [pause 31]
reflect1: O taste and see. Faith is not only something you think. It is something you can taste, like bread, like water when you are thirsty. [pause 35]
reflect1: Two more verses from later in the same psalm. [pause 29]
reflect2: Near to the brokenhearted. If your heart is broken tonight, he is not far. He is nearer than you think. [pause 33]
reflect2: Here is the beginning of Psalm 34 again. Let it taste like something good. [pause 35]
rest: rest5

=== pn-13-psalm-103 | Psalm 103: Bless the Lord, O My Soul
main: PSA 103:1-14
companions: LAM 3:22-23
welcome: Hello. This is Psalm Nights. [pause 12]
welcome: Settle in. Let your breath slow. [pause 15]
welcome: Tonight we read Psalm 103, the first fourteen verses. It is a psalm where a man talks to his own soul and reminds it of everything God has done. [pause 14]
welcome: We will read it slowly, and then once more. [pause 15]
reflect1: Bless the Lord, O my soul. Sometimes we have to speak to our own soul. Remind it. Forget not all his benefits. [pause 20]
reflect1: The psalm makes a list. He forgives. He heals. He redeems. He crowns with lovingkindness. These are not promises about tomorrow. They are a memory of who God has been. [pause 22]
reflect1: Then comes a picture of distance. East from west, that is how far he has carried our sins away. You cannot walk to where the east ends. [pause 20]
reflect1: And the last verse tonight is very gentle. He knoweth our frame. He remembers that we are dust. He knows how tired you are. He is not surprised. [pause 24]
reflect1: A companion passage from Lamentations. [pause 18]
reflect2: His compassions fail not. They are new every morning. Whatever was used up today, there will be new mercy when you wake. [pause 22]
reflect2: Here is Psalm 103 again. Let your soul listen. [pause 24]
rest: rest1

=== pn-14-psalm-63 | Psalm 63: In the Night Watches
main: PSA 63:1-8
companions: PSA 42:8; PSA 119:148
welcome: Welcome to Psalm Nights. [pause 15]
welcome: Find a comfortable way to lie. Let the pillow hold your head. [pause 15]
welcome: Tonight's psalm is Psalm 63. David wrote it in the wilderness of Judah, in a dry land with no water. And in that dry place he wrote about lying awake at night, thinking of God. [pause 15]
welcome: If you are awake tonight, this one is for you. We will read it twice. [pause 16]
reflect1: O God, thou art my God. The psalm does not start with a request. It starts with a name. My God. [pause 24]
reflect1: My soul thirsteth for thee. In a dry land, where no water is. Some nights feel like that. Dry. Empty. Far from what you need. [pause 24]
reflect1: Then verse six. Remembering God upon his bed, thinking of him through the night watches. A night watch was a part of the night, the way a guard counted the hours. David spent his sleepless hours remembering God. [pause 26]
reflect1: And joy, under the shadow of his wings. [pause 28]
reflect1: Two short companion verses about the night. [pause 22]
reflect2: His song, in the night. A song in the night. [pause 24]
reflect2: Mine eyes prevent the night watches. That means my eyes are open before the watchman calls, so I can think on your word. If you cannot sleep, you can still rest your mind on him. [pause 26]
reflect2: Here is Psalm 63 again. If your eyes are open, let the words be your night watch. [pause 28]
rest: rest2

=== pn-15-psalm-42 | Psalm 42: As the Hart Panteth
main: PSA 42:1-11
companions: PSA 43:5
welcome: Hello, and welcome to Psalm Nights. [pause 8]
welcome: Let yourself be still. You do not have to feel any particular way tonight. [pause 11]
welcome: Tonight we read Psalm 42. It is a psalm for low nights. The writer is far from home, sad, and honest about it. He talks to his own soul and asks it why it is so cast down. [pause 10]
welcome: We will read it twice. [pause 13]
reflect1: The hart, panting for the water brooks. A deer, tired and thirsty, looking for a stream. That is what longing for God can feel like. [pause 7]
reflect1: Tears, day and night, for food. There is no pretending here. Some seasons, tears are what you live on. The Bible makes room for that. [pause 9]
reflect1: Then the question, twice. Why so cast down, my soul? And the answer, twice. Hope thou in God. Not because the sadness is gone. Because God is still God. [pause 9]
reflect1: Verse eight is for tonight. His song is with me in the night. [pause 11]
reflect1: One companion verse, from the next psalm. [pause 5]
reflect2: Psalm 43 ends with the same words. As if the writer had to say it to himself again and again. Hope in God. I shall yet praise him. [pause 9]
reflect2: If you are low tonight, you do not have to feel better before you rest. And if the sadness stays for many days, tell someone. A pastor, a doctor, a friend. That is part of hoping too. [pause 9]
reflect2: Here is Psalm 42 again. [pause 11]
rest: rest3

=== pn-18-psalm-90 | Psalm 90: Our Dwelling Place
main: PSA 90:1-12

welcome: Let your body be heavy. Let your hands rest. [pause 11]

welcome: We will read it twice. [pause 13]
reflect1: Our dwelling place, in every generation. Before your grandparents. Before their grandparents. God has been home to his people all along. [pause 17]
reflect1: A thousand years to God are like yesterday, like a watch in the night. A watch in the night. To God, all of history is like a few hours of the dark. [pause 19]
reflect1: The psalm is honest about how short life is. Like grass in the morning, gone by evening. And so it asks for wisdom. Teach us to number our days. [pause 19]
reflect1: Two more lines from the end of the psalm. [pause 15]
reflect2: Satisfy us early with thy mercy. A prayer for the morning. [pause 17]
reflect2: And establish the work of our hands. Whatever you did today, small or large, you can ask God to make it count. [pause 19]
reflect2: Here is Psalm 90 again. Let the long time of God hold your short night. [pause 21]

=== pn-20-psalm-125 | Psalm 125: As the Mountains Are Round About
main: PSA 125:1-5
companions: PSA 121:5-8; DEU 33:27
welcome: Welcome to Psalm Nights. [pause 17]
welcome: Find your place. Let the covers settle over you. [pause 20]
welcome: Tonight's psalm is Psalm 125. It is short, one of the songs pilgrims sang on the way to Jerusalem. It is a psalm about mountains, and about being surrounded. [pause 19]
welcome: We will read it slowly, and then again. [pause 22]
reflect1: Those who trust the Lord are like mount Zion, which cannot be moved. A mountain does not move in the wind. [pause 32]
reflect1: As the mountains are round about Jerusalem. The city sat in a ring of hills. When you stood in its streets, you could see the mountains on every side. [pause 32]
reflect1: So the Lord surrounds his people. On every side. Behind you. Ahead of you. Around this bed tonight. [pause 36]
reflect1: Two companion passages. [pause 30]
reflect2: From Psalm 121, the Lord is your shade at your right hand. And from Deuteronomy, a verse many have leaned on. Underneath are the everlasting arms. [pause 34]
reflect2: Underneath. As you lie down, you can picture that. Arms that hold you from below. [pause 34]
reflect2: Here is Psalm 125 again. Think of the mountains all around. [pause 36]
