# Imagination Nights 11-20 (English, Mindset door). Written by Claude in our own words (no free-model draft, no quotes from any book), with Claude's safety and cultural read. No faith words or links; no outcome promises. Uses the shared settles and closes from 001-010.

=== in-11-waking-up | Waking Up Inside It
theme: the morning after
settle: settle1
teaching: Tonight we try something a little different. Instead of picturing the evening after your wish, we picture the morning. [pause 6]
teaching: The first few seconds after waking are honest. Before we remember the day, the body already knows how it feels. Some mornings we wake up with a knot. Other mornings we wake up light. [pause 8]
teaching: Tonight you will imagine waking up some morning after your wish has come true. Not the day it happened. A plain morning, weeks later, when it has become part of your life. [pause 8]
teaching: Notice how your body feels in that first moment. What is the first thought? Where is the light coming from? What do you hear? [pause 8]
teaching: As always, this is a practice of imagination and feeling. It is not a promise about what will happen. It is a gentle way to go to sleep with a lighter picture of tomorrow, and to let your body rehearse what ease feels like. [pause 8]
teaching: Bring your wish to mind now. Just name it once, quietly. [pause 13]
scene: It is early morning. You are just waking up. Your eyes are still closed. [pause 15]
scene: Notice the sounds. A bird outside. A car far away. The hum of the house. [pause 18]
scene: Before you even open your eyes, you remember. Oh. That is right. It already happened. [pause 18]
scene: Feel the smile come before you decide to smile. [pause 18]
scene: Open your eyes in the scene. Notice the light on the ceiling. The color of the walls. [pause 18]
scene: Stretch. Feel how easy your body is this morning. No dread sitting on your chest. [pause 18]
scene: Swing your feet onto the floor. Feel it, cool and solid. [pause 18]
scene: Walk to the kitchen and start the coffee or the kettle. Hear it begin. [pause 21]
scene: While you wait, look out the window. The day ahead is ordinary, and that is the best part. Your wish is simply part of the ordinary now. [pause 21]
scene: Someone texts good morning. You text back. Life is moving, and it is moving well. [pause 21]
scene: Pour your cup. Hold it with both hands. Feel the warmth. [pause 23]
scene: Say quietly in your mind, this is my life now. I am glad. [pause 23]
scene: Then let the morning fade, and let the night come back around you, carrying that morning inside it. [pause 28]
close: close1

=== in-12-the-letter | The Letter You Open
theme: good news arriving
settle: settle2
teaching: Tonight's scene is about opening good news. A letter, an email, a message. The moment you read the words you hoped for. [pause 13]
teaching: Many of us have opened news that disappointed us. We remember that feeling, and we brace for it again. So tonight we rehearse the other feeling. The quick breath. The second read, just to be sure. The laugh that slips out. [pause 15]
teaching: The trick is to make the moment small and specific. Not a whole story. Just your hands, the screen or the paper, and a few words you can almost see. [pause 15]
teaching: You choose what the news is. An acceptance. An approval. A yes. Keep it private if you like. Nobody else needs to know what you picture. [pause 15]
teaching: If you have opened a lot of hard news lately, go slowly. You can picture the envelope for a while before you open it. Let your breathing settle first. There is no rush in imagination. [pause 5]
teaching: Remember, this is a practice of imagination. It is not a promise of any result. It is a way to let your body practice relief instead of dread. [pause 20]
scene: You are sitting somewhere you often sit. A kitchen chair. The edge of the bed. Your car before you go inside. [pause 22]
scene: A message comes in. You see who it is from. Your stomach flutters. [pause 25]
scene: Open it. Let your eyes find the first line. [pause 25]
scene: There it is. The word you hoped for. Read it slowly. [pause 25]
scene: Read it again, just to be sure. It still says the same thing. [pause 25]
scene: Feel the breath leave your body all at once. Feel your hand go to your mouth, or your heart. [pause 28]
scene: Maybe you laugh. Maybe your eyes fill. Let whichever one comes come. [pause 28]
scene: Now picture who you tell first. Hear yourself say it out loud for the first time. It happened. [pause 28]
scene: Hear them answer. Hear the joy in their voice. [pause 28]
scene: Later that night, you read the message one more time before bed. It feels calmer now. Real. [pause 30]
scene: Set the phone down. Lie back. Let the words stay in your mind as you drift. [pause 30]
close: close2

=== in-13-the-family-table | The Family Table
theme: family
settle: settle1
teaching: Tonight's wish is about family. For some people that means peace between relatives. For others it means children doing well, or parents cared for, or a new family made of friends. [pause 10]
teaching: Families are made of people with their own choices, so this practice is never about forcing anyone to change. We picture the feeling of a table where people are kind to each other, and where you feel at ease. [pause 12]
teaching: Food and laughter, a long meal, nobody watching the door. You can fill in the faces you want, or leave them soft and blurry. The feeling is the point. [pause 12]
teaching: If your family has hurt you, you do not have to picture those people. You can picture the family you are building now, the people who are good to you. [pause 12]
teaching: This is imagination and feeling, not a promise. It is a way to rest tonight with a warm picture instead of an old argument. [pause 17]
scene: It is late afternoon on a day off. You are at a table with people you love. [pause 19]
scene: Notice what is on the table. Plates passed hand to hand. Something you made. Something someone else brought. [pause 22]
scene: Hear the noise. Two conversations at once. A child laughing somewhere. A chair scraping. [pause 22]
scene: Someone tells an old story, and everyone groans because they have heard it before, and then they laugh anyway. [pause 22]
scene: Notice that nobody is tense. There is no old fight waiting under the surface. [pause 25]
scene: Someone reaches over and squeezes your shoulder as they pass. [pause 22]
scene: Look around the table, one face at a time. Let yourself feel glad for each one. [pause 25]
scene: Later, people stand at the door saying goodbye for a long time, the way families do. [pause 25]
scene: When the house is quiet again, you sit for a moment with a full heart. That was a good day. [pause 27]
scene: Say in your mind, we are all right. We are together. [pause 27]
scene: Let the table and the voices fade, and keep the warmth. [pause 30]
close: close1

=== in-14-the-trip | The Trip You Took
theme: travel and rest
settle: settle2
teaching: Tonight's scene is about a trip. Somewhere you have wanted to go. Maybe the ocean. Maybe a city. Maybe the town where your grandparents were born. [pause 13]
teaching: A trip is a good wish to practice with, because the details are so easy to feel. Sand. Wind. A new street. Food that tastes different. [pause 15]
teaching: Instead of picturing the planning and the packing, we picture being there, on the third or fourth day, when you have already relaxed into it. [pause 15]
teaching: You do not have to know how the trip would happen. That is daytime work. Tonight is only for the feeling of having arrived, of being rested and unhurried. [pause 15]
teaching: Let the place be as real as you can make it. Picture the weather, the sounds of the language people speak there, the shoes you are wearing. Small details make the scene feel like a memory instead of a wish. And memories are restful. [pause 5]
teaching: As always, this is imagination, not a promise. Even if the trip stays a dream for now, the rest you feel tonight is real. [pause 20]
scene: You wake up in a room that is not your own, and for a second you do not know where you are. Then you remember, and you smile. [pause 25]
scene: Open the curtains. Notice what is outside. Water. Hills. Rooftops. [pause 25]
scene: Step outside. Feel the air on your face. It smells different here. Salt, or pine, or bread from a bakery. [pause 25]
scene: Walk slowly. You have nowhere to be. Your phone is in your pocket, quiet. [pause 25]
scene: Find a place to sit. A bench. A wall. A patch of sand. Watch people going about their morning. [pause 28]
scene: Taste something new. Let it surprise you. [pause 25]
scene: Notice that your shoulders are lower than they have been in months. [pause 28]
scene: Later, the light turns gold. You are tired in the good way, from walking and looking. [pause 28]
scene: Back in the room, you lie down, and through the window you hear the sounds of this place settling into night. [pause 30]
scene: Say in your mind, I came. I am here. [pause 30]
scene: Let those far-off sounds become the sounds around you now. [pause 30]
close: close2

=== in-15-finishing | The Day You Finished
theme: finishing something hard
settle: settle1
teaching: Tonight is for anyone working toward a finish line. A degree. A certificate. A book. A long project. Paying something off. Getting through a hard season. [pause 11]
teaching: When we are in the middle of something long, the end can feel unreal. We see only the next assignment, the next shift, the next payment. Tonight we step forward to the finish. [pause 13]
teaching: Picture the day it is done. Not the stress of the last week, but the moment you know it is over. Maybe you are holding a paper. Maybe you just close a laptop. Maybe someone hugs you. [pause 13]
teaching: Let yourself feel proud. Many of us are not used to that feeling. It is all right to practice it. [pause 13]
teaching: If you have started and stopped before, that is not a mark against you. Many people finish on the second or third try. Tonight you can picture this time as the time you get to the end. [pause 5]
teaching: This is imagination and feeling, not a promise of any result. Keep doing the work in the daytime. Tonight, rest with the end in view. [pause 18]
scene: It is the day it is finished. Notice where you are. A hall. An office. Your own kitchen table. [pause 20]
scene: Feel what is in your hands. A diploma. The last page. The final receipt. [pause 23]
scene: Notice the quiet in your chest where the pressure used to be. [pause 23]
scene: Someone near you says, you did it. Hear the words. Let them land. [pause 23]
scene: Think back for a moment to the hardest night of the whole thing. The one where you almost quit. [pause 23]
scene: Now look at yourself today, on the other side of it. Let yourself be a little amazed. [pause 26]
scene: Picture going home that evening. Taking off your shoes. Nothing due tomorrow. [pause 26]
scene: Someone calls to congratulate you. You hear yourself say, thank you. I am really proud. [pause 26]
scene: Before bed, you put the proof of it somewhere you will see it. On the fridge. On a shelf. [pause 28]
scene: Say quietly, I finished. [pause 28]
scene: Let that sentence be the last one in your mind tonight. [pause 30]
close: close1

=== in-16-at-peace-with-yourself | At Peace with Yourself
theme: self-worth
settle: settle2
teaching: Tonight's wish is not about getting something. It is about how you feel about yourself. [pause 16]
teaching: Many of us talk to ourselves at night in ways we would never talk to a friend. We list our mistakes. We call ourselves names. We wonder why we are like this. [pause 18]
teaching: Tonight we practice a different voice. We picture a version of you, some months from now, who has made peace with who you are. Not perfect. Just kind to yourself, steady and unashamed. [pause 18]
teaching: If the voice in your head has been very hard on you for a long time, or if you have thoughts of hurting yourself, please talk to someone in the daytime. In the United States you can call or text 988 at any hour. You deserve help and you deserve kindness. [pause 18]
teaching: This is imagination and feeling, not a promise. But practicing a kind voice is a good way to end the day. [pause 23]
scene: You are standing in front of a mirror, some months from now. [pause 25]
scene: Look at your own face. Notice that you do not look away. [pause 28]
scene: Notice your eyes. They look rested. [pause 28]
scene: Hear yourself say, in your mind, good morning. And mean it. [pause 28]
scene: Picture yourself making a small mistake later that day. Spilling something. Forgetting a name. [pause 28]
scene: Hear how you speak to yourself now. That is all right. Everybody does that. You laugh a little and move on. [pause 30]
scene: Picture someone giving you a compliment. Notice that you can say thank you without arguing. [pause 30]
scene: In the evening, picture sitting quietly by yourself. It feels like good company. [pause 30]
scene: Say softly, I am enough as I am, and I am still growing. [pause 30]
scene: Let that kind voice stay with you, like a hand on your back, as you drift. [pause 30]
close: close2

=== in-17-the-open-sign | The Open Sign
theme: your own business
settle: settle1
teaching: Tonight's wish is for anyone who dreams of running something of their own. A shop. A salon. A food truck. A small online business. A practice. [pause 11]
teaching: When you start something, most of the picture is worry. Money, customers, rules, time. Tonight we step past all that, to a normal working day when the thing is up and running and people are coming. [pause 13]
teaching: We are not picturing fame or riches. We are picturing ordinary success. A steady line of people. A regular who knows your name. Enough. [pause 13]
teaching: This is a practice of imagination and feeling, not a promise of any result. Keep doing the real work in the daytime, and get good advice when you need it. Tonight is for the feeling of having built something. [pause 13]
teaching: If you already run something and it is struggling, you can use this scene too. Picture a good, ordinary day a few months from now, when things have steadied. Picture the bills paid and the regulars coming back. Let your body remember that a business can feel calm. [pause 5]
teaching: Picture your business now, however it looks to you. [pause 18]
scene: It is early morning. You are unlocking the door of your place, or opening your laptop to your shop. [pause 20]
scene: Turn on the lights. Notice the first thing you see. Your name. Your colors. Your things in their places. [pause 23]
scene: Flip the sign to open, or press the button that says live. [pause 23]
scene: The first person of the day arrives. They smile. They have been here before. [pause 23]
scene: Hear them say, I told my sister about you. She is coming next week. [pause 23]
scene: Feel yourself doing the work you are good at. Your hands know what to do. [pause 26]
scene: Midday, there is a little rush. It is busy in the good way. [pause 26]
scene: At closing, you count up the day. It was a good day. Not wild. Just good. Enough to feel steady. [pause 26]
scene: Flip the sign back to closed. Stand for a moment in the quiet room you built. [pause 28]
scene: Say in your mind, this is mine. I made this. [pause 28]
scene: Let the quiet of that room become the quiet of this one. [pause 30]
close: close1

=== in-18-revising-an-old-memory | Revising an Old Memory
theme: revision of the past
settle: settle2
teaching: Tonight we use revision again, but on an older memory. A moment from your past that still stings when you think about it. [pause 14]
teaching: Choose something small and mild. A time you were embarrassed. A time you did not speak up. A goodbye that went badly. Please do not choose a memory of being badly hurt or abused. Those memories deserve care from a trained person, not a bedtime exercise. If one comes up, set it gently aside for now. [pause 16]
teaching: In revision, you replay the old moment in your imagination, but you let it go the way you wish it had. You are not lying to yourself about what happened. You are giving your mind a kinder picture to rest on, and practicing the person you are now. [pause 16]
teaching: This is imagination and feeling, not a promise about anything outside you. It is simply a way to set down an old weight before sleep. [pause 21]
scene: Go back to that moment. Notice how old you were. Notice the place. [pause 23]
scene: Let the moment begin as it began. Then, just before it went wrong, pause it. [pause 26]
scene: Picture yourself today stepping into the scene, beside your younger self. [pause 26]
scene: Put a hand on their shoulder. Say, I am here. We can do this differently. [pause 26]
scene: Now let the moment play on in a new way. Hear your younger self speak up calmly. Or see the other person respond kindly. Or simply see yourself walk away with your head up. [pause 29]
scene: Let it end gently. [pause 26]
scene: Look at your younger self. Tell them, you did your best. I am proud of you. [pause 29]
scene: Play the new version once more, from the start, so it becomes familiar. [pause 29]
scene: Then let the scene fade, like a photograph left in the sun. [pause 30]
scene: Say quietly, that is finished. I can rest. [pause 30]
scene: Let the old weight go, and let your body be light. [pause 30]
close: close2

=== in-19-the-evening-walk | The Evening Walk
theme: a calm body
settle: settle1
teaching: Tonight's scene is simple. A walk at the end of the day, feeling calm and at home in your own body. [pause 12]
teaching: We are not picturing a change in your health or your body, and this practice makes no promise about either. Those belong with your doctor and your daily care. What we picture is how it feels to move through an evening without hurry, breathing easily, noticing the world. [pause 14]
teaching: A slow walk is one of the oldest ways people settle their minds. Even imagining one can slow the breath and soften the shoulders. [pause 14]
teaching: Choose a place you would like to walk. A neighborhood street. A park. A path by the water. It can be real or made up. [pause 14]
teaching: If walking is hard for you, picture a gentle way of moving that suits your body. Rolling down a smooth path. Sitting on a porch while the evening moves around you. The ease is what matters, not the steps. [pause 5]
teaching: Let the feeling of an easy evening be the whole wish tonight. [pause 20]
scene: You step outside. The day's heat has gone. The air is soft. [pause 22]
scene: Feel your feet on the ground. One, then the other. No hurry. [pause 24]
scene: Notice the sky. The last color in the west. The first star. [pause 24]
scene: Hear the sounds around you. A dog somewhere. Leaves. Someone's music through an open window. [pause 24]
scene: Breathe in the evening air. Let it out slowly. [pause 24]
scene: Pass a neighbor. You nod. They nod back. It is that kind of evening. [pause 28]
scene: Notice how your arms swing. How easy each step is. [pause 28]
scene: Stop for a moment and look at something small. A flower by a fence. A puddle holding the sky. [pause 28]
scene: Turn toward home. The windows are lit. Yours is one of them. [pause 30]
scene: Say in your mind, I am at ease. [pause 30]
scene: Step inside, and let the walk carry you all the way to bed. [pause 30]
close: close1

=== in-20-a-year-from-now | A Year from Now
theme: living in the end, the long view
settle: settle2
teaching: Tonight we finish this set with a longer view. A year from now. [pause 13]
teaching: Picture yourself a year from tonight, lying in bed, thinking back over the year. The wishes you carried this year have had time to grow. Some of them are part of your ordinary life now. [pause 15]
teaching: In this practice you look back, not forward. You remember the year as if it has already happened. You notice how you changed. How you got through the hard parts. What surprised you. [pause 15]
teaching: Looking back from the end is the heart of living in the end. It lets your mind rest in the finished picture instead of the unfinished road. [pause 15]
teaching: You do not have to fill in every month. Let your mind skip around, the way memory does. A smell, a face, a moment of laughter. Whatever comes up is enough. [pause 5]
teaching: As always, this is imagination and feeling, not a promise. Real life will have its own turns. But tonight you can fall asleep looking back on a good year. [pause 20]
scene: It is a year from tonight. You are lying in bed, much like now. [pause 22]
scene: Notice the room. Is it the same room, or a different one? [pause 25]
scene: Let your mind drift back over the year. Start with the spring. What happened? [pause 25]
scene: Then the summer. A day you remember. A meal. A trip. A conversation. [pause 25]
scene: Then the fall. Something you finished. Something you let go. [pause 25]
scene: Then the winter. A quiet night, much like this one. [pause 28]
scene: Now think of the wish you have carried this year. Picture it as part of your life now. Ordinary. Familiar. [pause 28]
scene: Notice how you feel about the person you were a year ago. Be kind to them. They were doing their best. [pause 28]
scene: Say in your mind, it was a good year. I am glad I kept going. [pause 30]
scene: Let the whole year rest in your chest, like a long breath out. [pause 30]
scene: And let the year from now and this night become one quiet night. [pause 30]
close: close2
