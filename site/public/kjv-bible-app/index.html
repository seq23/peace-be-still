KJV Bible app: what a good King James Bible app should have

A KJV Bible app is a phone app that holds the King James Version, the English translation first printed in 1611. Because the KJV is in the public domain in the United States, any app can carry the whole text free. What differs between apps is what sits around it: audio, ads, trials, and what is locked behind a subscription.

Quick answer

Check five things: the whole KJV is free to read, every verse shows its book, chapter and verse, there is audio, there are no ads, and nothing starts as a trial that turns into a charge. Peace Be Still, coming soon, has the whole KJV free to read, the Psalms and more chapters read aloud, no ads and no trial.

What this page is best for: people who read the King James Version and want an app for it, or who were asked to pay for a Bible they expected to be free.

Is the King James Bible free to use in an app?

Yes, in the United States. The King James Version is in the public domain there, so an app can include all 66 books at no cost to you or to the app maker. In the United Kingdom it is printed under a Crown patent, which is why some UK editions carry a licence line.

So if an app asks you to pay just to read the KJV, the charge is for the app, not for the text. Many good apps carry it free.

Why do people still read the King James Version?

Many readers grew up on it, many churches still read it from the pulpit, and its rhythm makes it easy to memorise and to hear aloud. It is the translation most hymns, sermons and old family Bibles quote.

Thy word is a lamp unto my feet, and a light unto my path.

Psalm 119:105 · KJV

The grass withereth, the flower fadeth: but the word of our God shall stand for ever.

Isaiah 40:8 · KJV
