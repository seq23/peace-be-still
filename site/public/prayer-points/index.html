Prayer points, declarations and night vigils, with the King James open

Peace Be Still is a coming-soon phone app for the start and the end of the day, built on the King James Version that many Nigerian and Ghanaian congregations still read from the pulpit. Today it has the whole KJV and a daily declaration that stands on one verse; an AI Bible chat arrives with the app's launch. A journey shaped around prayer points, declarations and the night vigil is being built for January 2027.

Quick answer

Today: the King James Bible to read and hear, a morning declaration from Scripture, and whispered Psalms at night. Bible chat arrives with the app's launch. Not yet: prayer points and a vigil order inside the app. Those are being built for January 2027, and this page will say when they are ready.

What this page is best for: Nigerian, Ghanaian and other African believers in the US who pray with prayer points, keep Friday vigils, and want the King James close at hand between services.

Who this is for

The sister who joins the 5 a.m. prayer line by phone before her shift. The fellowship that keeps a Friday vigil in somebody's living room in Houston, Dallas, Atlanta, Maryland or the Bronx. The father who leads family devotion before school, in English, Yoruba, Igbo, Twi or Ewe, with the KJV open on the table. The student who misses home church and wants the same Scripture on her phone.

Whatever church you belong to, Pentecostal, Anglican, Methodist, Presbyterian, Baptist or independent, Peace Be Still keeps the text of Scripture at the centre and leaves the preaching to your pastor.

Every declaration stands on a verse

In Peace Be Still, a declaration is Scripture said back to God, with its chapter and verse beside it, so you always know what you are standing on. The Bible and the declarations come from a stored King James text, never typed from memory.

Frequently asked questions

Does Peace Be Still have prayer points today?

Not yet. Today it has a daily declaration built on one verse and the whole KJV; Bible chat arrives with the app's launch. Prayer points and a vigil order are being built for January 2027.

Which Bible does it use?

The King James Version, read from a stored copy, with book, chapter and verse.

Is it for one church or denomination?

No. It keeps to the text of Scripture so believers from Pentecostal, Anglican, Methodist, Presbyterian, Baptist and independent churches can all use it. For your own situation, it points you to your pastor.

Is the chat a pastor or a prophet?

No. The Bible chat, arriving with the app's launch, is AI and will say so on every answer. It is not a pastor or a person; for your own situation, talk with your pastor.

Is it free?

Yes. The free lane stays free: the whole KJV to read and hear, today's story, one chat a day of up to 10 messages (chat arrives with the app's launch), your first sleep night and then one a week, and a daily declaration. The paid lane is $7.99 a month or $39.99 a year.
