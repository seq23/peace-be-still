What is a Bible chat?

It is a conversation with an AI about faith and scripture. You might ask what the Bible says about worry, how to pray for a friend, or what a hard passage means. The answer should be short, kind and rooted in named verses, and it should send you to your pastor for your own situation.

How accurate are Bible chat apps?

It varies, which is why the verse matters. YouVersion's founder and CEO, Bobby Gruenewald, said AI models misquote Scripture “at least 15% of the time,” and “Some of them as much as 60% of the time” (reported by Crosswalk, 18 Mar 2026). Those are his figures, not a test we ran.

So ask any Bible chat for a verse you know well, and compare it with your own Bible. The words should match a named translation, and the reference should be one that exists.

Every word of God is pure: he is a shield unto them that put their trust in him. Add thou not unto his words, lest he reprove thee, and thou be found a liar.

What should a Bible chat never do?

  • Make up a verse or a reference. If a chat cites a verse, you should be able to find it.
  • Speak as God or Jesus. “I am the Lord” from an app is not prayer. Peace Be Still's chat labels every answer as AI and never speaks as God or Jesus.
  • Dodge what Scripture plainly says. Where faithful churches genuinely differ, it should say so briefly; elsewhere it should answer from the plain words.
  • Promise money, healing or a result. It can pray with you and point you to scripture; it cannot promise outcomes.
  • Hide where your words go. Before your first free chat, Peace Be Still says it goes to a free AI service that may keep and use it, so leave out names.
