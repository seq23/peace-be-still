Private journal app: five checks before you write anything personal

A private journal app is one where your entries are stored only on your phone (or encrypted so the company cannot read them), are never used to train AI, and can be locked, exported and deleted by you. AI journal apps add a catch: anything you send to an AI feature leaves your phone, so its privacy depends on that AI service's rules.

Quick answer

Read the privacy policy for three things: where entries are stored, whether any text is sent to an AI service and whether that service may keep or learn from it, and how to delete everything. If the answer is unclear, assume it is not private.

What this page is best for: anyone choosing a journal or AI journal app for personal writing.

Five checks

  1. Storage: on your phone only, synced to your own cloud, or on the company's servers?
  2. AI features: which text goes to an AI service, and may that service keep it or use it to improve its models?
  3. Training: does the policy say plainly that your writing is never used to train AI?
  4. Lock: can you lock entries behind your phone's passcode or Face ID?
  5. Delete and export: can you get your words out, and delete them for good?

Where to look

The App Store's privacy label is a summary written by the developer. The privacy policy is the binding text: search it for “store”, “server”, “train”, “third party” and “delete”.

A simple rule

If you would not want it read aloud, keep it in a journal that stays on your phone, and keep it out of any free AI chat.

Frequently asked questions

Are AI journal apps private?

Only as private as the AI service they send your words to. Check whether that service may keep your text or use it for training.

Is a notes app a private journal?

If it stores notes only on your phone or in your own encrypted cloud, it can be. Check its sync settings.

What happens to my journal if I delete the app?

If entries are stored only on the phone, deleting the app deletes them. Export first if you want to keep them.
