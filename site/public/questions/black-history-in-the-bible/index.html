Black history in the Bible: what scripture records about Africa

The Bible records Africa at almost every stage: Egypt from Abraham to the Exodus, the kingdom of Cush (Kush) whose king Tirhakah marched against Assyria, Africans from Egypt, Libya and Cyrene at Pentecost, and an Ethiopian official baptised in Acts 8. Black history in America then took the Exodus story as its own.

Quick answer

Three threads: African nations inside the Bible (Egypt, Cush, Put, Cyrene), African people inside the church from day one (Acts 2:10, 8:27, 13:1), and the Bible inside Black American history, from the spirituals to the freedom movement.

What this page is best for: teachers, Bible study leaders and Black History Month planners who want sourced facts.

What does the Bible say about Black history?

The Bible does not tell “Black history” as a topic, but it is full of African history. Egypt is the setting for Joseph and the Exodus. The kingdom of Kush, south of Egypt, was a Black African power that ruled Egypt as its 25th dynasty, and one of its kings is named in scripture:

And when he heard say of Tirhakah king of Ethiopia, Behold, he is come out to fight against thee: he sent messengers again unto Hezekiah, saying,

2 Kings 19:9 · KJV

Africans in the church from the start

At Pentecost, the crowd that heard the apostles included people from Egypt and from Libya near Cyrene:

Phrygia, and Pamphylia, in Egypt, and in the parts of Libya about Cyrene, and strangers of Rome, Jews and proselytes,

Acts 2:10 · KJV

Within a few chapters an Ethiopian official is baptised (Acts 8), and by Acts 13 two of the five leaders in Antioch, Simeon called Niger and Lucius of Cyrene, have African ties.

A prophecy the Black church holds close

Psalm 68 says Ethiopia will reach out to God. Black preachers in America quoted this verse for two centuries as a promise about African people.

Princes shall come out of Egypt; Ethiopia shall soon stretch out her hands unto God.

Psalm 68:31 · KJV

How did the Bible shape Black history in America?

Enslaved Christians heard the Exodus as their story: a God who sees the suffering of slaves and says Let my people go. That is why the spirituals sing of Moses, Pharaoh and the Jordan. Many enslaved believers worshipped in secret in the woods, in what were called hush harbors.

And the LORD said, I have surely seen the affliction of my people which are in Egypt, and have heard their cry by reason of their taskmasters; for I know their sorrows;

Exodus 3:7 · KJV

Who was the first Black person in the Bible?

The first African name in the Bible's family lists is Cush, son of Ham (Genesis 10:6). The first African person whose story is told at length is Hagar the Egyptian (Genesis 16).

Frequently asked questions

Is Cush Africa in the Bible?

Yes. Cush is the land south of Egypt along the Nile, now mostly in Sudan, home of the kingdom of Kush. The King James Version usually translates it “Ethiopia”.

Which Pharaoh in the Bible was Black?

The Bible does not name a Black pharaoh. It does name Tirhakah, king of Ethiopia (2 Kings 19:9), whom historians identify with Taharqa, a Kushite king who ruled Egypt in the 25th dynasty.

Why is the Exodus so important in Black churches?

Because enslaved Christians heard in it a God who sees slaves and sets them free. The spirituals, the sermons and the freedom movement all drew on it.
