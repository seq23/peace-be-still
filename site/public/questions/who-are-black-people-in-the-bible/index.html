Who are Black people in the Bible? How readers tell, word by word

Black people in the Bible are found by following the places and words the text uses: Cush (translated “Ethiopia” in the KJV), Mizraim (Egypt), Put (usually Libya), and Niger, a Latin word for black used as a nickname in Acts 13:1. Lists online disagree because some add people the text never places in Africa.

Quick answer

Trust a name when the verse itself says Cushite, Ethiopian, Egyptian, Cyrenian or Niger. Treat everyone else on popular lists as a later reading, however beloved.

What this page is best for: people who have seen conflicting lists and want to know how to check one.

Who are Black people descended from in the Bible?

Genesis 10 lists Ham's sons as Cush, Mizraim, Phut and Canaan. The first three are African lands: Cush, Egypt and Libya. Readers have long linked African peoples to this list.

And the sons of Ham; Cush, and Mizraim, and Phut, and Canaan.

Genesis 10:6 · KJV

One warning. Genesis 9:25 puts a curse on Canaan, not on Ham and not on Cush. The claim that Black people carry a “curse of Ham” is not in the text, and it was used to defend slavery.

And he said, Cursed be Canaan; a servant of servants shall he be unto his brethren.

Genesis 9:25 · KJV

Who are some Black people in the Bible?

Named in the text as African: Moses's Cushite wife, Hagar the Egyptian, Ebed-melech the Ethiopian, Tirhakah king of Ethiopia, the Ethiopian treasurer, Simon of Cyrene, and Simeon called Niger. For the full table with each verse, see Black people in the Bible.

Why do lists online disagree?

  • Some lists add people because of later art or tradition, not the text.
  • Some treat any Egyptian or North African as “Black”, though ancient Egypt held many peoples.
  • Some rely on what a name sounds like in English.
  • Some miss that the KJV's “Ethiopia” is usually Cush, the land of the Kushite kingdom.

Frequently asked questions

What does Niger mean in Acts 13:1?

Niger is a Latin word meaning black or dark. Simeon “that was called Niger” was a teacher in the church at Antioch. Many readers take the nickname to describe his skin; the verse itself does not explain it.

Is Ethiopia in the Bible the same as Ethiopia today?

Usually not. In the King James Version “Ethiopia” translates Cush, the land along the Nile south of Egypt, now mostly in Sudan. The modern country took the name much later.

Is the curse of Ham in the Bible?

No. Genesis 9:25 curses Canaan, Ham's son, and says nothing about skin. The “curse of Ham” idea was read into the text and used to justify slavery.
