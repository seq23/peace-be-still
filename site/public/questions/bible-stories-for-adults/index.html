Bible stories for adults: 12 that hit differently as a grown-up

Bible stories for adults are the same stories children learn, read as a grown-up: Job's loss, Hannah's prayer for a child, Elijah wanting to give up, the father who runs to the prodigal son. They are best read straight from scripture, one story at a time, where the hard parts are left in.

Quick answer

Start with Elijah under the juniper tree (1 Kings 19:1-15) or the prodigal son (Luke 15:11-32). Each takes about five minutes to read aloud. Read the passage itself, not a summary.

What this page is best for: adults who grew up on Sunday school versions and want the stories again, in full, without cartoons.

Why read Bible stories as an adult?

Because most of them were never children's stories. Elijah asks to die under a tree, then sleeps and eats twice before God speaks to him. Hannah prays so hard the priest thinks she is drunk. These are stories about grief, family and fear, told plainly.

And as he lay and slept under a juniper tree, behold, then an angel touched him, and said unto him, Arise and eat.

1 Kings 19:5 · KJV

Are there Bible stories for adults to listen to?

Yes. The simplest way is to listen to the passage itself read aloud: any of the stories above takes five to ten minutes. Many free audio Bibles read the King James Version, which is in the public domain in the United States.

What is the best Bible story to fall asleep to?

Pick a quiet one, not a battle. Jesus calming the storm (Mark 4:35-41) is short and ends in a great calm. Ruth and Naomi and the road to Emmaus are gentle too. Leave Jericho and Goliath for the morning.

Frequently asked questions

What Bible stories are good for adults?

Stories with grown-up struggles: Job's grief, Hannah's longing, Elijah's exhaustion (1 Kings 19), the prodigal son and his older brother (Luke 15), Ruth and Naomi after loss, and Joseph forgiving his brothers.

Where do I start reading Bible stories as an adult?

Start with one short story read straight from scripture, such as Luke 15:11-32. Read it aloud once. Then read the story around it the next day.
