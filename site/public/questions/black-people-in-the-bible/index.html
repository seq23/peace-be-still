Black people in the Bible: who they are, with chapter and verse

Yes, there are Black and African people in the Bible. Scripture names Cushites (the King James Version usually says “Ethiopians”) such as Moses's wife, Ebed-melech who rescued Jeremiah, and the treasurer baptised in Acts 8, plus Africans from Egypt and Cyrene such as Hagar and Simon of Cyrene. The Bible does not sort people by race; it names their lands.

Quick answer

The clearest cases are the people the Bible calls Ethiopian or Cushite: Moses's wife (Numbers 12:1), Ebed-melech (Jeremiah 38:7), the Ethiopian official (Acts 8:27) and King Tirhakah (2 Kings 19:9). Add Simeon called Niger (Acts 13:1) and Simon of Cyrene (Mark 15:21).

What this page is best for: readers who want names and verses they can check, not a list of guesses.

Are there Black people in the Bible?

Yes. Cush, the land south of Egypt in what is now Sudan, appears dozens of times, and the King James Version mostly translates it as Ethiopia. The ancient kingdom of Kush was a Black African kingdom that for a time ruled Egypt itself. Jeremiah even uses the dark skin of Cushites as a known fact in a proverb:

Can the Ethiopian change his skin, or the leopard his spots? then may ye also do good, that are accustomed to do evil.

Jeremiah 13:23 · KJV

How many Black people are in the Bible?

Nobody can give an honest number. The Bible names people by land, tribe and family, not by colour, and ancient Egyptians, Cushites and Libyans ranged widely in appearance. What can be counted: Cush or Ethiopia appears well over fifty times in the King James Old Testament, and several named Cushites act at key moments in Israel's story.

Who was the first Black person in the Bible?

The first African name in scripture is Cush himself, listed among the sons of Ham in the table of nations, and his son Nimrod:

And the sons of Ham; Cush, and Mizraim, and Phut, and Canaan.

Genesis 10:6 · KJV

Whether to call Cush “the first Black person” depends on reading a family list as a race list, which the text does not do. It does show Africa in the human story from the start.

What does this mean for Black believers?

That Africa is not a footnote in the Bible. An African official is one of the first people baptised after Pentecost. An African man carried the cross. A Cushite saved a prophet's life and God sent him a promise by name (Jeremiah 39:15-18). These are stories the Black church has preached for generations.

Frequently asked questions

Is Cush Africa in the Bible?

Yes. Cush is the region south of Egypt along the Nile, in what is now Sudan, home of the ancient kingdom of Kush. The King James Version usually translates it as “Ethiopia”, which is not the same as the modern country of Ethiopia.

Who was the Ethiopian eunuch in Acts 8?

A senior official, treasurer to Candace, queen of the Ethiopians. Philip met him on the desert road while he was reading Isaiah, explained the passage, and baptised him (Acts 8:26-39).

Was Moses's wife Black?

Numbers 12:1 calls her an Ethiopian woman, Hebrew “Cushite”, from the African land of Cush. Miriam and Aaron spoke against Moses because of her, and God rebuked them.

Does the Bible talk about race?

Not in the modern sense. It names nations, lands and families. It also says God made all nations of one blood (Acts 17:26).
