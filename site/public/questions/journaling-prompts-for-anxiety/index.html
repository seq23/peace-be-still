Journaling prompts for worry: getting it out of your head

Journaling prompts for anxiety are questions you answer in writing when worry builds up, to get racing thoughts out of your head and onto paper, sort what you can and cannot control, and end on one small next step. Writing it down slows the loop and makes the worry something you can look at.

Quick answer

Set a ten-minute timer. Write the worry in full, then answer: what is in my control, what is not, and what is one thing I can do in the next 24 hours? Close the notebook when the timer ends.

What this page is best for: people whose worries loop at night or before big days.

Get it out

  1. What exactly am I worried about? Write all of it.
  2. What is the story my mind is telling about this?
  3. What is the worst, best and most likely outcome?
  4. What would I tell a friend who wrote this?

Sort it

  1. Which parts can I control, and which can't I?
  2. What is one thing I can do in the next 24 hours?
  3. Who could help with this?
  4. What can wait until tomorrow?

Affirmations are not medical care. If worry is getting in the way of your days, a doctor or a licensed counselor can help. If you are thinking about hurting yourself, call or text 988, the Suicide & Crisis Lifeline (United States), any time.

Close the loop

End every entry the same way, with one line such as “I have written it down. I can put it away until morning.” A fixed ending tells your mind the session is over. Writing earlier in the evening, not in bed, keeps the bed for sleep.

Frequently asked questions

Is journaling good for anxiety?

Many people find that writing a worry down makes it feel smaller and easier to sort. It is a helpful habit, not medical care.

When is the best time to journal about worry?

Early evening often works, so the worry is out of your head before bed rather than in it.

What if journaling makes me feel worse?

Keep it short, use the sorting prompts, and always end on one next step. If worry is constant, talk to a doctor or counselor.
