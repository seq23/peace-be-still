A prayer for sleep you can pray tonight

A prayer for sleep is a few honest words to God at the end of the day: hand over what you are carrying, ask for rest, and lie down. Try this one tonight: “Lord, I give you today. Keep me and the people I love tonight. I lie down in your peace.” Then let Psalm 4:8 be the last thing you read.

Quick answer

Keep it short enough to say with your eyes closed. Thank God for one thing, hand over one worry by name, ask for rest, and end with Psalm 4:8. If your mind keeps running, say the same line again instead of starting a new one.

What this page is best for: anyone lying awake who wants words to pray, not a long list to read.

What is a short prayer to say before bed?

Three you can learn by heart. Pick one and keep it for a week.

  1. For a heavy day: “Father, today was a lot. I put it in your hands. I am not carrying it into my sleep. Amen.”
  2. For a worried mind: “Lord, you know what I am afraid of. You are awake while I sleep. Keep my house tonight. Amen.”
  3. For someone you love: “Jesus, hold my mother (or my son, my friend) tonight wherever they are. Give us both rest. Amen.”

None of these are magic words. They are a way of telling God the truth and then lying down.

What does the Bible say about sleep?

The Bible treats sleep as a gift and a sign of trust. David slept in the middle of a war and said the Lord kept him:

I laid me down and slept; I awaked; for the LORD sustained me.

Psalm 3:5 · KJV

Psalm 127 goes further and says that worrying late into the night does not add anything:

It is vain for you to rise up early, to sit up late, to eat the bread of sorrows: for so he giveth his beloved sleep.

Psalm 127:2 · KJV

Which Psalm is for sleeping in peace?

Psalm 4. It is an evening psalm, and its last verse is the one most people pray at bedtime: I will both lay me down in peace, and sleep: for thou, LORD, only makest me dwell in safety. Read the whole psalm once, then keep only verse 8 in your mouth as you settle. See six psalms for sleep if you want more.

Is it okay to fall asleep while praying?

Yes. Many believers fall asleep mid-prayer, and there is nothing in scripture that counts it against you. The disciples fell asleep in Gethsemane, and Jesus woke them gently. If you drift off while talking to God, that is a good place to drift off.

Praying when your mind will not stop

  • Say one line, slowly, on the out-breath. Repeat it rather than adding new ones.
  • Name the worry out loud once, then stop discussing it. You have told God; you do not need to tell him nine times.
  • If a task comes to mind, write it on paper by the bed and go back to the line.
  • If you would rather listen than speak, play a psalm read softly with a sleep timer.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best prayer for sleep?

The one you will actually pray. Short is better: thank God for one thing, hand over one worry, ask for rest. Many people end with Psalm 4:8: I will both lay me down in peace, and sleep: for thou, LORD, only makest me dwell in safety.

Can I pray for sleep if I feel anxious?

Yes. Philippians 4:6 tells believers to bring everything to God in prayer, “with thanksgiving”. A prayer is not a treatment; if anxiety or poor sleep goes on for weeks, talk to a doctor as well as to God.

Should I pray out loud or silently at night?

Either. A whisper can help you slow down and stay with the words, especially if you share a room. Silent prayer is just as much prayer.

How long should a bedtime prayer be?

A minute is plenty. A long prayer often turns into a list of worries. Say what matters, end with a verse, and let yourself rest.
