Bible verses for grief: scripture for the loss of a loved one

Bible verses for grief are the passages people turn to after someone they love has died: words that say God is near the broken-hearted, that Jesus wept at a graveside, and that death does not have the last word. They are not a way to hurry grief. They are company for it, read slowly, one at a time.

Quick answer

The verse most people reach for first is Psalm 34:18, The LORD is nigh unto them that are of a broken heart; and saveth such as be of a contrite spirit. Others are Matthew 5:4, John 11:35, Psalm 147:3, 1 Thessalonians 4:13-14 and Revelation 21:4. Read one a day, aloud if you can.

What this page is best for: anyone who has lost someone and wants scripture for the hard days, and anyone looking for verses to send a grieving friend.

Which Bible verses help most in the first days after a loss?

In the first days, short verses are easiest to hold. These say that God is close, and that tears are not a lack of faith.

The LORD is nigh unto them that are of a broken heart; and saveth such as be of a contrite spirit.

Psalm 34:18 · KJV

Blessed are they that mourn: for they shall be comforted.

Matthew 5:4 · KJV

Jesus wept.

John 11:35 · KJV

John 11:35 is the shortest verse in the King James Bible. Jesus stood at the grave of his friend Lazarus and wept, even knowing what he was about to do. Grief is not something to hide from God.

What does the Bible say about a broken heart?

Scripture speaks of the broken-hearted often, and always with God moving toward them, not away.

He healeth the broken in heart, and bindeth up their wounds.

Psalm 147:3 · KJV

Blessed be God, even the Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, the Father of mercies, and the God of all comfort; Who comforteth us in all our tribulation, that we may be able to comfort them which are in any trouble, by the comfort wherewith we ourselves are comforted of God.

2 Corinthians 1:3-4 · KJV

Yea, though I walk through the valley of the shadow of death, I will fear no evil: for thou art with me; thy rod and thy staff they comfort me.

Psalm 23:4 · KJV

What does the Bible say about seeing loved ones again?

For Christians who grieve a believer, Paul writes that grief and hope sit together: we sorrow, but not as people with no hope.

But I would not have you to be ignorant, brethren, concerning them which are asleep, that ye sorrow not, even as others which have no hope. For if we believe that Jesus died and rose again, even so them also which sleep in Jesus will God bring with him.

1 Thessalonians 4:13-14 · KJV

Let not your heart be troubled: ye believe in God, believe also in me. In my Father’s house are many mansions: if it were not so, I would have told you. I go to prepare a place for you. And if I go and prepare a place for you, I will come again, and receive you unto myself; that where I am, there ye may be also.

John 14:1-3 · KJV

And God shall wipe away all tears from their eyes; and there shall be no more death, neither sorrow, nor crying, neither shall there be any more pain: for the former things are passed away.

Revelation 21:4 · KJV

Which verses are for the long nights and the anniversaries?

Grief often comes back at night, on birthdays and on the date of the loss. These are for those hours.

For his anger endureth but a moment; in his favour is life: weeping may endure for a night, but joy cometh in the morning.

Psalm 30:5 · KJV

It is of the LORD’s mercies that we are not consumed, because his compassions fail not. They are new every morning: great is thy faithfulness.

Lamentations 3:22-23 · KJV

For I am persuaded, that neither death, nor life, nor angels, nor principalities, nor powers, nor things present, nor things to come, Nor height, nor depth, nor any other creature, shall be able to separate us from the love of God, which is in Christ Jesus our Lord.

Romans 8:38-39 · KJV

A short prayer for the grieving

Lord, you are near to the broken-hearted, and my heart is broken. I do not have the words tonight, so I bring you the tears. Thank you for the life of the one I miss. Hold what I cannot hold, and stay close in the hours that are hardest. In Jesus' name, amen.

A prayer written for this page, not a verse of scripture.

How do I use these verses when I am grieving?

  1. One verse a day. Write it on a card and keep it where you will see it.
  2. Read it aloud. Hearing the words in your own voice helps them land when reading is hard.
  3. Pray it back. Turn the verse into a sentence to God: “You are nigh unto me, and my heart is broken.”
  4. Let someone in. Send one verse to a friend who is grieving too, or to someone who asked how to help.
  5. Talk to your pastor. A church grief group or your pastor can walk with you in ways a page cannot.

If grief feels like too much

Grief can be heavy enough that you think about not going on. If that is you, please reach out now: in the United States, call or text 988 (the 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline, 24 hours, free). Outside the US, findahelpline.com lists a free line near you. Reaching out is not a failure of faith.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best Bible verse for grief?

Psalm 34:18 is the verse most people turn to first: the LORD is nigh unto them that are of a broken heart. Matthew 5:4, John 11:35 and Revelation 21:4 come next. The best one is the one you can carry today.

What Bible verse can I send to someone who lost a loved one?

Keep it short and gentle: Psalm 34:18, Matthew 5:4 or 2 Corinthians 1:3-4. Add one line of your own, such as “I am praying for you this week,” and do not expect a reply.

Is it a lack of faith to grieve?

No. Jesus wept at the grave of Lazarus (John 11:35), and Paul tells believers not to sorrow even as others which have no hope, which means sorrow is expected. Faith and grief live in the same heart.

What does the Bible say about death of a loved one?

For believers, Scripture speaks of death as sleep and of a resurrection to come (1 Thessalonians 4:13-14), of a place prepared (John 14:2), and of a day with no more death, sorrow or crying (Revelation 21:4).
