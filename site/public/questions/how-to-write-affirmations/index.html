How to write affirmations you actually believe

To write an affirmation, put one thing you want to think or do into a short sentence that starts with “I”, uses the present tense, and is small enough that you can say it and mean it. Then test it out loud and trim it until it sounds like you.

Quick answer

Write what you want (“be calmer at work”), turn it into a present-tense “I” sentence (“I stay calm at work”), make it smaller and concrete (“I take one breath before I reply to a hard email”), and say it out loud. Keep the version that does not make you wince.

What this page is best for: people who want to write their own lines instead of borrowing generic ones.

Five steps

  1. Name the change in plain words.
  2. Start with “I” and use the present tense.
  3. Make it believable: shrink it until you could say it is true today.
  4. Add an action you can do in the next 24 hours.
  5. Say it out loud. Cut any word you would never say in real life.

Common mistakes

  • Future tense (“I will…”) keeps it far away.
  • Negatives (“I am not scared”) keep the word “scared” in your head; say what you are instead.
  • Too many lines: one you use beats ten you forget.

Frequently asked questions

Should affirmations be in the present tense?

Yes, usually. Present tense describes you now, so you can act on it today.

How long should an affirmation be?

Under fifteen words is a good limit, short enough to remember without reading it.

Can I use other people's affirmations?

Yes, as a start. Change a word or two so it fits your life.
