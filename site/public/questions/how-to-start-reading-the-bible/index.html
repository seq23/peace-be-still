How to start reading the Bible: a simple way in for beginners

To start reading the Bible, begin with one of the Gospels, the four books about the life of Jesus, rather than at page one. Read one chapter a day, at the same time each day, and write one line about what you noticed. The Bible is a library of 66 books, so it helps to know which shelf you are reading from.

Quick answer

Start with Mark (16 short chapters) or John (21). Read one chapter a day, ask three questions (what does this say about God, about people, about me?), and keep going after a missed day. John says why the Gospels were written: But these are written, that ye might believe that Jesus is the Christ, the Son of God; and that believing ye might have life through his name.

What this page is best for: new believers, people coming back to faith, and anyone who has tried to read the Bible from Genesis and stalled.

Where should a beginner start reading the Bible?

Start with a Gospel. Mark is the shortest and moves fast; John slows down and explains who Jesus is. After that, many people read Psalms (prayers and songs) and Proverbs (short sayings, one chapter for each day of the month), then Acts (the first churches) and Genesis.

The beginning of the gospel of Jesus Christ, the Son of God;

Mark 1:1 · KJV

How do I read the Bible every day?

  1. Pick a time. The same time each day: with coffee, at lunch, or before bed.
  2. Pick an amount. One chapter is enough. Ten minutes is enough.
  3. Read slowly, then once more. The second read is where things stand out.
  4. Ask three questions. What does this show about God? About people? What will I carry today?
  5. Write one line. A sentence in a notebook or a notes app.
  6. Miss a day? Keep going. Pick up at the next chapter, not back at the start.

Which translation should I read?

Choose one you will actually read, and stay with it for a while. The King James Version (1611) is the classic: many churches read it, most hymns and old family Bibles quote it, and it is in the public domain in the United States. Its older words (thee, thou, hath) take a week or two to get used to; reading it aloud helps.

Thy word is a lamp unto my feet, and a light unto my path.

Psalm 119:105 · KJV

What if I don't understand what I read?

That is normal, even for long-time readers. Mark the verse and keep reading; the next chapter often explains it. Ask your pastor or a small group, and read with someone if you can. The Bible itself pictures people searching it together and checking what they heard:

These were more noble than those in Thessalonica, in that they received the word with all readiness of mind, and searched the scriptures daily, whether those things were so.

Acts 17:11 · KJV

What else do people ask?

What book of the Bible should I read first?

Mark or John. Mark is short and fast; John explains who Jesus is. Starting at Genesis is fine too, but many people stall in Leviticus, so a Gospel first is the easier way in.

How long does it take to read the whole Bible?

About a year at three or four chapters a day. There are 1,189 chapters in a Protestant Bible. One chapter a day takes a little over three years, and that is fine.

Should I read the Bible in order?

Not at first. The books are grouped by kind, not by the order you need them. Start with a Gospel, then Psalms, Proverbs, Acts and Genesis, then fill in the rest.

Is the King James Version hard to read?

It uses older English, but most readers adjust in a week or two. Reading it aloud, and starting with a Gospel or the Psalms, makes it easier.
