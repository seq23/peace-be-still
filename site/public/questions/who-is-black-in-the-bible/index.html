Who is Black in the Bible? Seven people the text itself calls African

The people the Bible itself calls African are Hagar the Egyptian, Moses's Cushite wife, Cushi the messenger, Ebed-melech the Ethiopian, Tirhakah king of Ethiopia, the Ethiopian treasurer in Acts 8, and Simon of Cyrene. Each one is named by land in the text, so you can check it yourself.

Quick answer

Seven names you can open and read: Genesis 16, Numbers 12, 2 Samuel 18, Jeremiah 38, 2 Kings 19, Acts 8 and Mark 15.

What this page is best for: someone who wants the stories behind the names, told short.

Hagar the Egyptian

An Egyptian servant in Abraham's house who ran into the desert, pregnant and alone. God met her by a spring, and she became the only person in scripture who gives God a name.

And she called the name of the LORD that spake unto her, Thou God seest me: for she said, Have I also here looked after him that seeth me?

Genesis 16:13 · KJV

Moses's Cushite wife

Miriam and Aaron spoke against Moses because he had married a Cushite woman. God answered by rebuking them, not her (Numbers 12:1-15).

Cushi, the runner

The man who carried the news of Absalom's death to King David is called Cushi, “the Cushite” (2 Samuel 18:21-32). He ran the hardest message in the story.

Ebed-melech the Ethiopian

When Jeremiah was dropped into a muddy pit to die, a Cushite official at the king's court went to the king, got permission, and pulled him out with ropes and old rags so they would not cut him. God later sent Ebed-melech a promise by name.

For I will surely deliver thee, and thou shalt not fall by the sword, but thy life shall be for a prey unto thee: because thou hast put thy trust in me, saith the LORD.

Jeremiah 39:18 · KJV

Tirhakah, king of Ethiopia

Named in 2 Kings 19:9 and Isaiah 37:9 as marching against Assyria. Historians identify him with Taharqa, a king of Kush who ruled Egypt in the 600s BC.

The Ethiopian treasurer

A court official of Candace, queen of the Ethiopians, reading Isaiah aloud in his chariot. Philip explained the passage, and the man asked to be baptised by the road.

And as they went on their way, they came unto a certain water: and the eunuch said, See, here is water; what doth hinder me to be baptized?

Acts 8:36 · KJV

Simon of Cyrene

A man from Cyrene in North Africa, coming in from the country, made to carry Jesus's cross (Mark 15:21). Mark names his sons, Alexander and Rufus, as if the first readers knew them.

Who is the ancestor of Black people in the Bible?

Genesis 10 lists Cush, Mizraim (Egypt) and Phut among the sons of Ham, and readers have linked African peoples to them. The Bible does not present a race ancestor, and it says God hath made of one blood all nations of men (Acts 17:26).

Frequently asked questions

Was the Queen of Sheba Black?

The Bible does not say. Sheba is often placed in southern Arabia (today's Yemen); Ethiopian tradition calls her Makeda and holds her as an Ethiopian queen. 1 Kings 10 tells her visit to Solomon.

Was Hagar Black?

Genesis calls her an Egyptian. Ancient Egypt held people of many complexions, so the text gives her nation, not her colour. Many Black readers have long seen their own story in hers.

Who is Ebed-melech in the Bible?

A Cushite official in King Zedekiah's court who rescued the prophet Jeremiah from a pit (Jeremiah 38:7-13). God promised to deliver him because he trusted in God (Jeremiah 39:15-18).
