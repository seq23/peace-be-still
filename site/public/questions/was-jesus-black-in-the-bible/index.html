Was Jesus Black? What the Bible says, and what it does not

The Bible does not say what colour Jesus's skin was. It says he was a Jewish man from Nazareth in Galilee, in the first century, and it describes no feature of his face. The pale, blond Jesus of many paintings is not from the Bible either; it came from the artists who painted him.

Quick answer

Short answer: the Gospels give no physical description. Revelation 1:14-15 describes the risen Christ in a vision with hair white like wool and feet like burning brass, which some read as a clue; it is a vision of glory, not a portrait.

What this page is best for: people who have heard both “Jesus was Black” and “Jesus was white” and want to see what the text says.

What did Jesus look like according to the Bible?

The four Gospels never describe his face, height, hair or skin. Isaiah's prophecy, which Christians read as about Jesus, says there was nothing striking about his looks:

For he shall grow up before him as a tender plant, and as a root out of a dry ground: he hath no form nor comeliness; and when we shall see him, there is no beauty that we should desire him.

Isaiah 53:2 · KJV

What does Revelation 1 say about his hair and feet?

John sees the risen Christ in a vision:

His head and his hairs were white like wool, as white as snow; and his eyes were as a flame of fire; And his feet like unto fine brass, as if they burned in a furnace; and his voice as the sound of many waters.

Revelation 1:14-15 · KJV

Many Black readers point to like wool and to feet like unto fine brass. Others note that the same verse says his hair was white as snow and his eyes like fire: this is a picture of glory and age, not of ethnicity. Both readings agree on one thing: it is not the Jesus of a stained-glass window in northern Europe.

Who was Jesus, by family and place?

A Jew of the tribe of Judah and the line of David, raised in Nazareth, a town in Galilee. Matthew's family line includes Tamar, Rahab (a Canaanite) and Ruth (a Moabite). He would have looked like other Jewish men of his time and region, not like a European.

And Salmon begat Booz of Rachab; and Booz begat Obed of Ruth; and Obed begat Jesse;

Matthew 1:5 · KJV

Why are there Black pictures of Jesus?

Because Christians everywhere have painted Jesus looking like their own people: in Ethiopia, in Europe, in Latin America, in Asia. A Black Jesus in a picture is a way of saying “he came for us too”, the same thing a European painting says for its people.

Frequently asked questions

Does the Bible say Jesus had hair like wool?

Revelation 1:14 says His head and his hairs were white like wool, as white as snow. In context it describes the colour (white), in a vision of the risen Christ. It is not a description of Jesus during his life.

Was Jesus white?

Not in the sense of a European. He was a Jewish man from Galilee in the first century. The fair-skinned Jesus of many paintings reflects the artists, not the Bible.

Is it wrong to picture Jesus as Black?

Christians across the world have pictured Jesus in their own likeness for centuries. The Bible gives no picture to break. What matters is who he is, not a portrait.
