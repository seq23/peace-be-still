Is there an African American Bible? The editions and the apps

An African American Bible is a Bible edition with notes, art and essays from the African American church, around the same scripture text. The best known are the African American Jubilee Edition (American Bible Society, 1999, KJV or CEV) and the Original African Heritage Study Bible (KJV, edited by Cain Hope Felder). The scripture itself is not changed.

Quick answer

If you want a printed Bible, look at the African American Jubilee Edition or the Original African Heritage Study Bible, both available in the King James Version. If you want an app, look for one that quotes a named translation word for word and speaks in a voice you trust.

What this page is best for: people shopping for a Bible, or a Bible app, made with African American readers in mind.

What is the African American Bible?

There is no separate African American translation. What exists are editions that wrap a standard translation in material about the African American faith story: articles, history, art, and notes on Africa and African people in scripture.

Why do many Black churches use the King James Version?

For many families it is the Bible of their grandparents' church, the words of the spirituals and the old sermons. Its rhythm is made for reading aloud. And it is in the public domain in the United States, so anyone can print, read and record it freely.

The LORD is my shepherd; I shall not want. (Psalm 23:1)

How do I choose an edition?

  1. Pick the translation first. If your church reads the King James Version, both editions above come in it.
  2. Decide how much study material you want. A study Bible has notes on nearly every page; a Jubilee-style edition keeps most essays in separate sections.
  3. Check the print size if it is a gift for an older reader.
  4. Look at the art. Many families choose these editions for pictures that look like them.

Frequently asked questions

Is there an African American Bible?

Yes, as editions rather than a new translation. The African American Jubilee Edition (1999) and the Original African Heritage Study Bible both use the King James Version with added notes, essays and art.

Who edited the African Heritage Study Bible?

Cain Hope Felder, a New Testament scholar at Howard University School of Divinity, was the general editor.

Is the scripture different in an African American Bible?

No. The scripture is the translation named on the cover, such as the KJV. The difference is in the notes, art and essays around it.
