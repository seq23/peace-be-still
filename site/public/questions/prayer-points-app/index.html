Is there a prayer points app? What to look for

A prayer points app gives you short, specific prayers to work through, ideally each tied to a Bible verse. When choosing one, check four things: that every point shows its chapter and verse, that the Bible translation is named, that no ads interrupt prayer, and that no free trial quietly turns into a yearly charge.

Quick answer

Look for chapter and verse on every point, a named translation, no ads, and the price in dollars before you tap. Peace Be Still does not have prayer points yet; they are being built for January 2027.

What this page is best for: people who pray with prayer points and want them on their phone without ads or surprise charges.

1. Every point stands on a verse

A prayer point is strongest when you can see what it stands on. Check that the app shows the chapter and verse for every point, and that the verse is quoted, not paraphrased.

Call unto me, and I will answer thee, and shew thee great and mighty things, which thou knowest not.

Jeremiah 33:3 · KJV

2. The translation is named

An app should say which Bible it quotes. The King James Version is in the public domain, so any app can quote it word for word; modern translations need the publisher's permission.

3. No ads in the middle of prayer

Free apps often pay for themselves with ads. An ad between prayer points breaks the flow of a vigil. Read the listing and reviews before you rely on one.

4. The price before you tap

Many apps open with “7 days free” and a yearly charge after. Check the price, the renewal date and how to cancel. On iPhone you can cancel any time in Settings › your name › Subscriptions.

Frequently asked questions

Is a prayer points app a replacement for church?

No. It is a help for the days between services. Keep your church's vigils and prayer meetings.

Can I write my own prayer points?

Yes, and many people do. Take one verse, turn it into one sentence of prayer, and write it down. That is a prayer point.

Which Bible does Peace Be Still use?
