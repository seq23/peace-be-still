Bible verses for sleep and anxiety: what to read when worry keeps you up

When anxiety keeps you awake, pair one verse that hands over the worry with one that lets you lie down. A good pair: 1 Peter 5:7, Casting all your care upon him; for he careth for you. then Psalm 4:8, I will both lay me down in peace, and sleep. Read the first, pray, then rest on the second.

Quick answer

Lights low. Read 1 Peter 5:7 and say your worry out loud once. Read Philippians 4:7. Lie down with Psalm 4:8 on repeat in your head. If you wake later, use Psalm 63:6 instead of picking up your phone.

What this page is best for: anyone whose worry gets louder at bedtime and who wants a routine, not a list.

Why does worry get louder at night?

The day's noise stops and there is nothing left to distract you, so the unfinished things come forward. The Psalms know this well. David wrote about being troubled at night and about meditating on God in the night watches. Night worry is old and common; it is not a sign that your faith is weak.

Thou holdest mine eyes waking: I am so troubled that I cannot speak.

Psalm 77:4 · KJV

A four-step night routine with scripture

  1. Hand it over.

    Casting all your care upon him; for he careth for you.

    1 Peter 5:7 · KJV
  2. Say it once. Name the worry out loud, in one sentence, to God.
  3. Receive peace.

    And the peace of God, which passeth all understanding, shall keep your hearts and minds through Christ Jesus.

    Philippians 4:7 · KJV
  4. Lie down.

    I will both lay me down in peace, and sleep: for thou, LORD, only makest me dwell in safety.

    Psalm 4:8 · KJV

Is there a verse for worry at night?

Psalm 42:8 was written by someone in deep distress, and it puts God's song in the night:

Yet the LORD will command his lovingkindness in the daytime, and in the night his song shall be with me, and my prayer unto the God of my life.

Psalm 42:8 · KJV

What to read if you wake at 3 a.m.

Keep the phone face down. Turn to one of these instead, from memory if you can:

  • When I remember thee upon my bed, and meditate on thee in the night watches. (Psalm 63:6)
  • Behold, he that keepeth Israel shall neither slumber nor sleep. (Psalm 121:4)
  • Thou wilt keep him in perfect peace, whose mind is stayed on thee: because he trusteth in thee. (Isaiah 26:3)

How do I pray when I am anxious at bedtime?

Short and honest. “Lord, I am worried about the rent (the test, my son, the doctor's call). I cannot fix it tonight. I give it to you until morning.” Then stop. The aim is not to solve it in prayer; it is to put it down for the night.

When to get more help

This is a faith practice, not medical care. If worry or poor sleep goes on for weeks, see a doctor. If you are thinking about harming yourself, call or text 988 now.

Frequently asked questions

What verse should I read when I can't sleep because of worry?

Start with 1 Peter 5:7 to hand the worry over, then lie down with Psalm 4:8: I will both lay me down in peace, and sleep: for thou, LORD, only makest me dwell in safety.

What does the Bible say about overthinking at night?

Psalm 94:19 speaks to it: In the multitude of my thoughts within me thy comforts delight my soul. Isaiah 26:3 promises perfect peace to the one whose mind is stayed on thee.

Is it wrong to take sleep medicine if I pray?

No. Prayer and medical care go together. Talk to your doctor about medicine; pray about everything, including that.

How many verses should I read at night?

Fewer than you think. Two verses held slowly are easier to rest in than a page read quickly. Keep the same pair for a week.
