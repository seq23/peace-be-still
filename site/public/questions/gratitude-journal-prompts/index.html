Gratitude journal prompts: 25 for ordinary and hard days

Gratitude journal prompts are short questions that help you notice and write down specific things you are thankful for. They are most useful when they push you past the obvious (“my family”) to a particular moment, person or detail from today.

Quick answer

Each night, answer one prompt in two or three sentences. Name something specific and why it mattered. Five minutes is enough.

What this page is best for: people starting a gratitude journal, or restarting one that went stale.

For any day

  1. What was the best ten minutes of today?
  2. Who made today a little easier, and how?
  3. What did I eat or drink today that I enjoyed?
  4. What is something in my home I am glad to have?
  5. What did my body let me do today?
  6. What made me laugh?
  7. What is a small comfort I usually overlook?
  8. What went better than I expected?
  9. Who would I like to thank, and for what?
  10. What did I learn today?

For hard weeks

  1. What got me through today?
  2. Who checked on me, or who could I reach out to?
  3. What is one thing that did not go wrong?
  4. What am I proud of handling?
  5. What can I let go of tonight?
  6. What is one thing I am looking forward to, however small?
  7. What is a kindness someone showed me, even a stranger?

For mornings, and the long view

  1. What am I glad I get to do today?
  2. What is something from last year I am grateful for now?
  3. Which friendship am I thankful for, and why?
  4. What did I once wish for that I now have?
  5. What skill am I glad I learned?
  6. What place makes me feel calm?
  7. What advice am I grateful someone gave me?
  8. What would I want to remember about this season?

Frequently asked questions

How often should I write in a gratitude journal?

Daily is ideal, but three times a week that you actually keep is better than daily that you abandon.

How many things should I write?

One to three, with a sentence on why each mattered.

What if I write the same things every day?

Use a prompt to push for a new detail, or write why the same thing mattered today in particular.
