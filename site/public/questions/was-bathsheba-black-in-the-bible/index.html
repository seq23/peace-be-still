Was Bathsheba Black? What the Bible tells us about her

The Bible does not say Bathsheba was Black. 2 Samuel 11:3 calls her the daughter of Eliam and the wife of Uriah the Hittite. She married King David, was the mother of Solomon, and is named in Jesus's family line in Matthew 1:6. No verse describes her skin.

Quick answer

Bathsheba was an Israelite woman, daughter of Eliam (2 Samuel 11:3), likely the granddaughter of David's adviser Ahithophel (2 Samuel 23:34). The idea that she was Black comes from later readings, not the text.

What this page is best for: people who have heard the claim and want what the verses actually say.

Who was Bathsheba in the Bible?

And David sent and enquired after the woman. And one said, Is not this Bath-sheba, the daughter of Eliam, the wife of Uriah the Hittite?

2 Samuel 11:3 · KJV

She is first seen from David's roof. David took her, then had her husband killed in battle. She later became David's wife, and the mother of Solomon, and she secured Solomon's place as king (1 Kings 1).

Who was Bathsheba's family?

Her father Eliam appears in the list of David's mighty men as the son of Ahithophel, David's counsellor:

Eliphelet the son of Ahasbai, the son of the Maachathite, Eliam the son of Ahithophel the Gilonite,

2 Samuel 23:34 · KJV

That makes her, very likely, an Israelite from a family close to the king.

Where does the idea that Bathsheba was Black come from?

Some readers link her to the Song of Solomon, where a woman says she is black and comely; the Song is about Solomon's beloved, not his mother. Others note that 1 Chronicles 3:5 calls her Bath-shua and connect the name to other women. None of these makes the text say she was Black.

Why is she in Jesus's family line?

Matthew names four women before Mary: Tamar, Rahab, Ruth, and her that had been the wife of Urias. Each has a hard story. Matthew includes them on purpose; grace runs through the family line.

And Jesse begat David the king; and David the king begat Solomon of her that had been the wife of Urias;

Matthew 1:6 · KJV

Frequently asked questions

Was Bathsheba a Hittite?

Her husband Uriah was a Hittite. Bathsheba herself is called the daughter of Eliam, and her family appears to be Israelite (2 Samuel 23:34).

Is Bathsheba the woman in the Song of Solomon?

No. The Song's speaker who says I am black, but comely is Solomon's beloved, not his mother Bathsheba.

Where is Bathsheba's story?

2 Samuel 11 and 12 (David and Uriah, Nathan's rebuke, Solomon's birth) and 1 Kings 1 and 2 (Solomon made king).
