Psalms for sleep: 6 bedtime psalms to read or hear tonight

Psalms for sleep are psalms people read or listen to at bedtime because they speak of rest, safety and trust in God. The most used are Psalm 4, 3, 23, 91, 121 and 127. Many people play one psalm read slowly, or whispered, with a sleep timer, so scripture is the last thing they hear before sleeping.

Quick answer

Start with Psalm 4: its last verse is “I will both lay me down in peace, and sleep.” Then try Psalm 23 or Psalm 91. Read or listen to one psalm, slowly, with the lights low.

What this page is best for: people who want a short list of bedtime psalms, quoted exactly, and a simple way to use them.

Which psalms are best to read before bed?

These six are the ones most often read at night, each with a verse from the King James Version:

Why is Psalm 4 called the bedtime psalm?

Psalm 4 is an evening prayer. David asks to be heard, tells the reader to “commune with your own heart upon your bed, and be still,” and ends with lying down in peace. That last verse has made it the traditional psalm for the end of the day.

How do I use the psalms for sleep?

  1. Choose one psalm for the night.
  2. Read it slowly, out loud or in your head, or play it read in a soft voice.
  3. Stay with one verse that stands out and repeat it as you settle.
  4. Use a sleep timer if you listen, so the audio fades out and nothing plays next.

Is Psalm 91 good to read at night?

Yes. Psalm 91 is one of the most-played psalms at bedtime; on YouTube, Psalm 91 sleep readings have gathered millions of views. It speaks of shelter “under the shadow of the Almighty” and of not being afraid “for the terror by night.”
