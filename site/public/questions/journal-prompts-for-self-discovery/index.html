Journal prompts for self-discovery: 30 questions in five themes

Self-discovery journal prompts are open questions that help you notice what you value, what drains or fills you, and what you want next. You answer them in writing, honestly and without editing, and look for patterns over a few weeks.

Quick answer

Pick one theme a week, answer one prompt a day for ten minutes, and at the end of the week reread your answers and underline anything that surprised you.

What this page is best for: people who want to understand themselves better and like thinking on paper.

Values and energy

  1. When did I last lose track of time, and what was I doing?
  2. What do I want people to say about me when I'm not in the room?
  3. What do I say yes to that I wish I said no to?
  4. What drains me fastest?
  5. What part of my day do I look forward to?
  6. What would I do with a free Saturday and no obligations?

People and fears

  1. Who do I feel most like myself around?
  2. Whose opinion do I worry about most, and why?
  3. What do I need from friends that I rarely ask for?
  4. What am I avoiding right now?
  5. What is the worst realistic outcome, and could I handle it?
  6. What did I believe about myself as a child that I still carry?

The future, and more

  1. What would a good ordinary day look like a year from now?
  2. What do I want more of, and less of?
  3. What small change would make the biggest difference?
  4. What would I try if I knew nobody would judge me?
  5. What am I ready to stop explaining?
  6. What did I get right this month?

Use the same six questions again in a month; changed answers are often the most useful part.

What else do people ask?

How long should I journal for self-discovery?

Ten minutes a day is enough. Write without editing; you are not writing for anyone else.

What if I don't know the answer?

Write “I don't know, but…” and keep going. The second sentence is often the interesting one.

Should I reread my journal?

Yes, weekly. Patterns only show up when you look back.
