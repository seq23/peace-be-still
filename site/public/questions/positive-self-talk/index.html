Positive self-talk: answering the voice in your head

Self-talk is the running commentary in your head. Positive self-talk does not mean pretending everything is fine; it means answering harsh or catastrophic thoughts the way a fair, encouraging coach would: accurately, kindly and with a next step.

Quick answer

Catch the thought (“I always mess this up”), check it (“Always? I got the last two right”), and replace it with something fair and useful (“I made one mistake; I can fix it this afternoon”).

What this page is best for: people who notice a harsh inner voice and want a practical way to talk back to it.

Catch, check, change

  1. Catch the thought. Write it down word for word.
  2. Check it like a fair friend would: is it true, all of it, every time?
  3. Change it to something accurate and useful, with one next step.

Examples of rewrites

Harsh thoughtFair self-talk
I'm useless.I'm struggling with this one task. I can ask for help with it.
Everyone thinks I'm annoying.I don't know what they think. Two people were glad to see me today.
I'll never get this done.I can do twenty honest minutes on it now.
I ruined the whole day.A bad hour is not a bad day.

Practise when it's easy

Rewrite one thought a day in writing, when you are calm. It builds the habit so that it is there when you are not calm. Saying one steady line out loud each morning gives you a ready-made answer.

Frequently asked questions

Is positive self-talk the same as affirmations?

Related. Self-talk is answering your own thoughts as they happen; affirmations are lines you choose in advance and repeat.

Should I talk to myself in the second person?

Some people find “You can do this” easier to hear than “I can do this.” Try both.

What if the harsh thought is true?
