Bedtime Bible stories: 10 gentle stories that end in peace

Bedtime Bible stories are short stories from scripture read or heard just before sleep. The best ones are calm and end in rest: Jesus calming the storm, the lost sheep carried home, Samuel hearing his name at night, the shepherds under the stars. Save battles and plagues for daytime.

Quick answer

Choose a story that ends quietly, read it in under ten minutes, then close with one verse from it. Jesus calms the storm (Mark 4:35-41) is the classic: it ends with a great calm on the sea.

What this page is best for: parents reading at bedtime, and adults who like to fall asleep to a story.

What is the best Bible story to fall asleep to?

Jesus calming the storm. It is short, it has a clear shape (wind, fear, one word from Jesus, quiet), and its last scene is the calm itself:

And he arose, and rebuked the wind, and said unto the sea, Peace, be still. And the wind ceased, and there was a great calm.

Mark 4:39 · KJV

Which Bible stories are too scary for bedtime?

Many children find these better in daylight: the flood itself (Genesis 7), the plagues of Egypt, David and Goliath, the walls of Jericho, Jonah inside the fish, and the crucifixion. They are not off-limits; they just make a busy last ten minutes before sleep.

How do I read a Bible story at bedtime?

  1. Read the story once, slowly, in a lower voice than usual.
  2. Ask one question, not a quiz: “What do you think Samuel felt?”
  3. End with one verse from the story, the same way every night.
  4. Pray, then lights out.

And the LORD came, and stood, and called as at other times, Samuel, Samuel. Then Samuel answered, Speak; for thy servant heareth.

1 Samuel 3:10 · KJV

Frequently asked questions

What Bible stories are good for bedtime?

Calm ones that end in rest: Jesus calms the storm (Mark 4), the lost sheep (Luke 15), Samuel hearing God at night (1 Samuel 3), the shepherds (Luke 2) and Moses in the basket (Exodus 2).

How long should a bedtime Bible story be?

Under ten minutes read aloud. Most of the stories above take two to five. A short story finished is better than a long one abandoned.

Should I read from a children's Bible or the real Bible?

Both work. A picture Bible helps small children follow. As they grow, read the passage itself; the King James Version is slow and rhythmic, which suits bedtime.
