The Watch Night service: history, order and a prayer for midnight

A Watch Night service is a church service held late on New Year's Eve that prays the old year out and the new year in at midnight. In Black American churches it carries the memory of Freedom's Eve, 31 December 1862, when people gathered to wait for the Emancipation Proclamation, which President Lincoln issued on 1 January 1863.

Quick answer

In 2026 Watch Night is Thursday 31 December. Most services start in the late evening with singing, testimony and the Word, and the church prays together as midnight comes. You can keep it at home with a psalm, thanks for the year, and prayer through midnight.

What this page is best for: anyone going to Watch Night for the first time, families keeping it at home, and anyone who wants to know where it came from.

Where did Watch Night come from?

Two streams meet in it. Methodists, following John Wesley, held watch nights from 1740: late services of prayer and Scripture, monthly at first and later on New Year's Eve, where Methodists also renewed their covenant with God. Then on 31 December 1862, enslaved and free Black people gathered in churches and homes to watch for the Emancipation Proclamation, which Lincoln issued the next day. That night is remembered as Freedom's Eve, and Black churches have kept Watch Night ever since.

The Proclamation freed people in the states then in rebellion; freedom reached many only as Union troops advanced, and slavery ended in law with the Thirteenth Amendment in 1865. Watch Night remembers the waiting as much as the news.

What happens at a Watch Night service?

Every church does it its own way. Most include:

  • congregational singing, often the old hymns and spirituals;
  • testimonies of what God did in the year that is ending;
  • Scripture and a sermon;
  • prayer through the last minutes of the year, often at the altar, into midnight;
  • in many churches, Holy Communion, and a fellowship meal after.

A Watch Night prayer for midnight

“Lord, we have watched and waited, and you have kept us. Thank you for every month of this year: for what you gave, and for what you carried us through. We remember the ones who waited before us and did not give up on you. We lay down what is behind us. Take this new year, our homes, our church and our children, and teach us to number our days. Amen.”

So teach us to number our days, that we may apply our hearts unto wisdom.

Psalm 90:12 · KJV

How do you keep Watch Night at home?

  1. Start around 11 p.m. with a hymn or spiritual everyone knows.
  2. Read Psalm 90 aloud, one verse each.
  3. Testify: each person names one thing God did this year.
  4. Lay down the old year. Pray over what is ending, by name.
  5. Pray through midnight, then welcome the new year with praise.
  6. Pray the new year in, with the prayer below, and eat together.

Frequently asked questions

Why do Black churches have Watch Night?

Black churches keep Watch Night in memory of Freedom's Eve, 31 December 1862, when enslaved and free Black people gathered to wait for the Emancipation Proclamation, issued on 1 January 1863. The service gives thanks for the year and prays the new one in.

What time does a Watch Night service start?

Times vary by church. Many start between 9 and 11 p.m. and run past midnight, so the congregation is praying as the year turns. Check your church's announcement or website for this year's time.

Is Watch Night the same as crossover night?

They are close cousins. Watch Night is the name in Black American and Methodist churches; many African churches call the same midnight service of prayer crossover night. Both thank God for the year that is ending and pray the new one in, and many families now keep both.

What do you wear to Watch Night?

Whatever your church usually wears on Sunday. Some churches dress up for the new year; others are casual because the service runs late. Bring a Bible and a coat, and something to write on if your church shares a word for the new year. Children are welcome in most churches.
