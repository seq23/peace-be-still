Daily Bible verse app: one verse a morning, and nothing in the way

A daily Bible verse app gives you one verse of scripture each day, usually in the morning, with its book, chapter and verse. The good ones get out of the way: the verse comes first, there are no pop-ups or ads in front of it, you can share it, and the reminder is one gentle nudge you chose, not a stream of notifications.

Quick answer

Look for a verse from a named translation with its reference, no ads, no pop-up before the verse, one-tap sharing of just that verse, and an optional reminder you can turn off. In Peace Be Still, now on the App Store, Morning Word gives a King James verse and a short declaration each morning, free.

What this page is best for: people who want a verse to start the day, without ads, upsells or notification guilt.

What should a daily Bible verse app do?

  1. Show the verse first. Nothing should stand between you and the morning's scripture: no gadget advert, no sponsor box.
  2. Name the translation and reference. “Lamentations 3:22-23 · KJV”, so you can find it in your own Bible.
  3. Let you share one verse. As text or a card, not a screenshot of the whole screen.
  4. Remind you gently, once. One optional reminder a day, off with one switch.
  5. Stay free. A verse a day should never need a trial or a subscription.

What does a morning verse look like?

Here is the first Morning Word in Peace Be Still, the King James text with its reference:

It is of the LORD’s mercies that we are not consumed, because his compassions fail not. They are new every morning: great is thy faithfulness.

Lamentations 3:22-23 · KJV

Under it sits one short declaration to say aloud, rooted in that verse: “Yesterday is behind me. This morning is new, and I get up leaning on a faithfulness that did not run out overnight.”

What do people dislike in daily verse apps?

In the 21 Nov 2026 read of 4,430 recent App Store reviews of eleven faith apps, the most common complaints in low ratings were price, features that used to be free moving behind a paywall, trials that turned into charges, and adverts or upsells. Some reviewers mention an advert appearing “the very second you open the app”. These are reviewers' reports about other apps, not a finding about any one of them.
